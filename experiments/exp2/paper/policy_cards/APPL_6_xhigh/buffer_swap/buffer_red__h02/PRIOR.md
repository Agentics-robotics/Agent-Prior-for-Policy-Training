# buffer_red__h02: recurrent contact-belief-conditioned action diffusion

## Assigned scientific hypothesis (unchanged)

The assigned heuristic is: **Attachment is a latent, persistent state: infer grasp/support changes from finger aperture together with object-TCP co-motion, rather than equating a close command with successful grasp. Hypothesis: contact-belief-conditioned diffusion reduces premature lifting or release and tolerates modest timing variation.**

This package implements candidate C, not a waypoint graph or a scripted contact controller. The original assignment is not modified. The engineering adaptations below specify what can actually be learned with the provided two-frame state interface and successful demonstrations. No improved robustness or task performance is asserted before training/evaluation.

## Scope, observation evidence and selection guidance

Use this model to vacate the initially red region: approach/pick up red, carry it to a supported free buffer, release, retreat empty, approach blue, close and begin lifting blue. It trains on **all** assigned expanded slices: demo10100 through demo10111 start at 0 and stop at 490, 487, 483, 488, 481, 480, 484, 493, 482, 486, 489 and 485, respectively (exclusive action stops; terminal observation retained). No sample, transition or overlap phase is removed or relabeled by trajectory time. Shared action ranges with transfer_blue are exactly those in the assignment, spanning roughly source steps 238--493 across demonstrations. The model is not trained here to complete blue transport or return red from the buffer to its final goal.

The actual states explain why contact inference differs from Cartesian location or command sign:

* demo10100:95 and 105 put TCP at almost the same z (0.02026 and 0.02024 m). Fingers change from 0.04 m each to 0.018267/0.018236 m while red is still at table height. The actions are +1 and -1, respectively. Subsequent red/TCP heights are about 0.0904/0.0906 m at 130 and 0.2603/0.2605 m at 150, with near-preserved relative translation. Finger instantaneous velocities remain nonzero even when aperture is nearly steady.
* At 240 red is still held above the buffer. At 265 red z=0.02283 m and fingers are at held width. At 270 fingers are about 0.03517 m each and at 275 about 0.03960 m each, with red supported at z=0.020 m. At 320 TCP z=0.30057 m but red stays at the buffer. Red position alone therefore does not identify whether release/retreat has finished.
* At 448 the blue approach has open fingers, at 455 the fingers are near 0.01825 m and blue is still near the table, and at 480 blue/TCP have risen together to about 0.08844/0.08861 m. At terminal state 490 blue z=0.18650 m and arm qvel is not zero; action 489 retains -1 grip.
* demo10111:95/105/140 reproduce the first close-and-lift relation. Its 266/276 reproduce red support/opening, and 448/475/485 reproduce blue closing and rising at different times and slightly different coordinates. The model uses no source index.

Measured endpoints across the twelve demonstrations have combined finger width around 0.03651 m, supported red near (-0.181, 0, 0.020) m, and blue z about 0.126--0.211 m. These are observed support, not applicability gates or learned-policy results. `HANDOFF.json` explains continuation versus transfer and cites the observations read during implementation. A geometric buffer subgoal can hold before manipulation is ready for transfer. Red support plus blue co-motion at held aperture is a useful exit cue; requiring a motionless arm would contradict the data. The task itself succeeds only by its supplied red_at_goal AND blue_at_goal contract, not an added release, speed, clearance or hold condition.

## Causal representation and normalization

Forward receives only noisy normalized actions, diffusion timestep and two raw causal observations [B,2,47]. The states include qpos, qvel, world TCP/red/blue poses, goals and the constant drawer compatibility channels; there are no images or forces. All 47 observations use the provided shared complete-demonstration mean/half-range normalizer. Native actions retain the shared affine encoding of seven absolute joint targets and the eighth gripper command. No per-skill normalizer is fitted or embedded.

A 91D feature vector per frame contains:

1. The 47 shared-normalized observations and two additional physical per-finger positions in metres (the same fingers are already represented in normalized qpos).
2. Two world-coordinate object-minus-TCP translations (6D), divided coordinatewise by the **sum** of the supplied TCP and corresponding object half ranges. This avoids amplifying blue x using a tiny skill-local range. There is no refit. Two TCP-relative rotation matrices (18D) are computed from normalized wxyz quaternions; using matrices avoids quaternion sign ambiguity.
3. Backward TCP/red/blue linear velocities (9D), backward relative translation velocities (6D) and backward aperture velocities (2D), using dt=0.05 s. Velocity scales are the same shared position scales per one-second reference interval: TCP half ranges for TCP motion, summed TCP/object half ranges for object and relative motion, shared finger half ranges for finger motion. The first frame's differences are zero with a zero-validity flag; the second frame carries the measured differences and a one-validity flag (1D).

Finger qvel remains available as part of the complete state, but it is not used as a hard grasp/failure threshold. Relative translation is world-expressed while relative rotation is TCP-relative; this is an explicit relational representation, not a claim of equivariant joint actions. There is no IK, forward-kinematics solver or rigid grasp constraint.

## Learned architecture and the memory adaptation

A 91->128->128 frame MLP with LayerNorm/SiLU feeds a 128D GRU over the two frames. The initial GRU state is a learned tanh projection of the first frame embedding, rather than an assumed empty/closed mode. The required interface has **only two frames and no recurrent-state argument**. Consequently this is a window-recurrent encoder: it reconstructs its belief for every forward call, including every DDPM evaluation. It does not accumulate hidden state across actions, invocations or sampling steps. This is a limitation relative to a long-running Bayesian/recurrent contact filter, not an undisclosed persistent-memory mechanism.

Learned emissions provide six mutually exclusive soft modes: empty (including open approaches/retreat), red-closing, red-attached, red-supported/releasing, blue-closing and blue-attached. An observation-conditioned MLP maps the two GRU outputs to a 6x6 transition matrix. Its logits have a trainable diagonal-initialized base (self logits 2, others 0); no transition is forbidden. First-frame emissions give p0; the transition gives p_pred = p0 T. Current belief is softmax(current_emission + 0.35 log(p_pred)). All these probabilities remain differentiable. This is a learned observation-conditioned filtering update, not a commanded phase sequence.

Two independent sigmoid support heads permit red support to coexist with blue attachment, which a single six-way mode could not express. A context MLP embeds final hidden state plus both normalized observations to 192 dimensions. Concatenating current six-way belief and two support probabilities yields a 200D denoiser condition. The raw-state skip allows metric joint/pose information to supplement rather than be discarded by a coarse belief bottleneck.

The public temporal conditional U-Net uses widths 128/256/512, kernel 5, timestep embedding 128 and eight groups. Its last projection is replaced by 64 learned features, followed by distinct learned linear projections to seven arm epsilon dimensions and one gripper epsilon dimension. Arm and gripper remain coupled through the temporal U-Net and belief. All eight outputs are epsilon predictions for the same fixed DDPM; no mode label generates or overwrites an action. The framework clips clean samples to the shared encoded range and decodes absolute commands, including gripper in [-1,1]. This bounds outputs by demonstrated shared action ranges; it is not a collision or contact guarantee. The PD contract's per-finger target is 0.025*command+0.015 m, so a commanded -1 is distinct from an observed held finger position around +0.0183 m.

## Training-only proxy labels

There are no direct attachment/contact labels. Under `no_grad`, targets are computed from observed poses, physical aperture, demonstrated gripper sign and at most eight valid subsequent states. This is supervision, never a forward input or runtime decision rule. Thresholds below are engineering choices, not calibrated success criteria.

For each pre-action state, an object is near TCP if its centre distance is below 0.055 m, and held-width means mean per-finger aperture in (0.012,0.028) m. Stable relative transform requires translation change below 0.006 m and relative-rotation-matrix Frobenius change below 0.10. Attachment confirmation requires either current centre z>0.035 m with a stable next relation, or a valid short future with z>0.035 m, rise>0.008 m and preserved relation and held aperture. The pre-state must also have held width, near TCP and a negative demonstrated command. Thus a close command by itself never defines attachment. A near-object close without confirmed attachment is a closing proxy.

Support targets require centre z<0.032 m and next-step object translation below 0.010 m. For the red-supported/releasing mode, red must additionally be displaced at least 0.10 m in xy from the initially red region (given by blue_goal) and within 0.070 m of TCP. That mode overrides red attachment near buffer support and spans pre-release and opening. Empty retreat becomes distinguishable as TCP moves away. Blue closing/attachment override the red modes when TCP reaches blue. These coarse labels deliberately do not claim to resolve actual force-bearing support, touching versus secure grip, or static contact.

Masks handle padding and skill ends. At observation t the action slots are t-1 through t+14 and future slot j is the state after its action. Thus pre-action states are [raw_history first frame, future_obs excluding its last slot]; pre slots 0 and 1 are the two encoder observations. Their labels use valid action/future masks. Pairwise belief losses require both frame labels valid; no future supervision crosses a slice boundary. Hindsight labels become less informative at short/padded ends. Attachment can be labeled shortly before visible lift based on successful future co-motion; this is a success-confirmed proxy, not causal access to the future at inference.

## Differentiable objectives and actual gradient paths

Let L_arm be mean masked epsilon MSE over the seven joints. Let L_grip be gripper epsilon MSE with masked, normalized weights 1 + 3 E + 1.5 C + R: E marks a five-slot neighborhood of a demonstrated sign change; C marks closing proxies; R marks red-supported/releasing. This balances short events through the loss, **not** a changed data sampler. Padding is excluded. Diffusion loss is (7 L_arm + 2 L_grip)/9. The gripper remains a continuous learned diffusion dimension.

The reported `prior_loss` is:

* **0.12 belief CE:** train first emitted and current filtered mode probabilities. Class weights [1,3,1.3,2,3,1.3] emphasize closing/releasing. Gradients reach emissions, GRU, frame representation, and, for the current posterior, transition parameters.
* **0.04 transition CE:** the learned transition row indexed by the earlier target predicts the later target, with fourfold weight for observed label changes. Gradients train transition logits and encoder.
* **0.02 transition/emission Jensen-Shannon consistency:** compare p0 T with current emission probabilities, retaining gradients through both.
* **0.01 unlikely transition mass:** penalize probability of red-attached->empty/blue-closing/blue-attached and blue-attached->empty/red-closing/red-attached. This is a weak soft regularizer, not an impossible-transition mask; red-attached->red-supported/releasing is allowed. All transition logits remain learnable. No penalty uses finger qvel as a failure trigger.
* **0.04 support BCE:** train the two per-frame support heads on observed support proxies. This is a loss on learned predictions, not a constant pose-only penalty.
* **0.08 teacher-action forecast and 0.04 estimated-action forecast**, as follows.

A separate action-conditioned 128D GRU is initialized from the 200D condition and consumes encoded candidate actions plus learned horizon-position embeddings. It predicts six changes in object-TCP relative translations, six changes in object world positions, two normalized finger positions and two attachment logits. Changes are measured relative to the current observation, normalized using the same shared derived translation scales. Attachment targets for after-action states require visible lift, held width, near TCP and stable one-step relation. Only the first eight valid future slots (current state through seven steps ahead) contribute. Each forecast loss is mean Smooth L1 over the 14 continuous predictions plus 0.25 BCE over attachment predictions.

The teacher branch sees demonstrated clean actions and grounds the predictive head. The estimated-action branch uses x0=(noisy_action-sqrt(1-alpha_bar)*epsilon)/sqrt(alpha_bar), clipped to [-2,2] **only for this auxiliary**. Its per-sample loss is multiplied by alpha_bar without normalizing away that suppression, limiting high-noise amplification. This branch differentiates through predicted actions into the denoiser and its gripper/arm projections as well as into the predictor/condition. The teacher branch trains the predictor and observation encoder. Neither branch receives future states as inputs: future states are masked targets only. There is no auxiliary that consists solely of observed-pose costs. The forecaster is a learned statistical model and might partly ignore actions; it is not verified physical dynamics or an enforcement of co-motion.

Total loss is diffusion_loss + prior_loss. Class/event weights and all auxiliary weights are fixed before training. No policy performance feedback is used to select them.

## Deployment, handoff and limitations

The framework performs the original 100-step epsilon DDPM sampling, horizon 16 and eight-action execution, with absolute native commands. Inference forward calls only the causal encoder and denoiser. No future labels, forecast rollout, demonstrated actions, source indices or time schedule are available to it. The learned transition matrix and belief influence the denoiser softly; there is no heuristic controller, replay, threshold-based output switch or hardcoded gripper reset. Internal posteriors are not part of the forward return, which is epsilon only.

At a mid-transition takeover, actual qpos/aperture, object location/support, TCP separation, qvel and backward co-motion let the same model distinguish a releasing red from a carried red, empty retreat from blue approach, and blue closing from initial blue rise. This supports learning continuation over the full overlap; it is not a proof of successful takeover under unseen timing. Do not reset the observation history at a skill index change. Give a successor the actual recent states, not an inferred ideal state. The blue rise at the observed exit supports continued -1 gripper while transfer_blue takes control; do not force a stationary arm or insert an open command. The successor can also enter earlier within shared support to finish acquisition.

Dependencies are Torch and the supplied `appl.public.DiffusionBackbone`; there is no external model, file access, sensor encoder or geometry engine. Use the unchanged training budget: seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW lr 1e-4/weight decay 1e-6, cosine with 500 warmup, gradient clip 1, EMA 0.999, last EMA selection. Shared normalization remains that fitted to all complete original demonstrations.

The evidence consists entirely of successful, accurately tracked demonstrations. Slips, occlusions, missed grasps and deliberate delays are not covered. Contact and support tolerances are unvalidated. Two-frame recurrence cannot encode long dwell history; in a static state different next commands may be observationally aliased. Posterior calibration and forecast accuracy must be assessed separately. Causal co-motion cannot establish force closure from stationary frames. Rotational inputs do not imply action equivariance, rotation-invariant task success or a hard grasp constraint. No recovery or delay tolerance is claimed. The scientific falsification test remains to ablate recurrent inference and co-motion supervision separately against a current-state diffusion policy and measure grip-event errors/premature opening, with delay perturbations only as a new evaluation, not an outcome of this implementation check.

# Deliver block h03: containment is not disengagement

## Assigned hypothesis and scope

The original assigned statement is: **Containment is not disengagement: learn release hysteresis and separate task-success and empty-hand readiness beliefs. A recurrent event-conditioned DP should preserve placed objects and avoid premature next-skill control even when the formal goal becomes true before fingers open.** This document describes an implementation of that hypothesis, not a modification to its source.

This package uses all 24 assigned M1_v2 expanded deliver_block slices, without trimming, changing boundaries, or relabeling the skill. The red slices include incoming acquisition overlap and outgoing lower/release/settle/retreat/blue-approach overlap. The blue slices include open acquisition through final open retreat/hold. There is no scripted subpolicy schedule. Every executed action is sampled by the fixed learned DDPM and decoded with the supplied shared action normalizer.

## Evidence and handoff interpretation

Actual observations/actions were read at demo10300 indices 75, 115, 145, 180, 265, 280, 285, 290, 300, 320, 360, 400, 414, 445, 495, 540, 640, 655, 660, 665, 680, 720, 741 and demo10307 indices 72, 100, 178, 259, 280, 287, 288, 292, 310, 335, 365, 407, 439, 470, 552, 632, 641, 646, 650, 659, 680, 700, 729. The assigned aggregate entry statistics show open fingers and TCP entry heights spanning approximately 0.088-0.178 m, not already-held payloads. The measured acquire_block overlaps include demo10300 [75,182), [265,415), [445,562) and demo10307 [72,178), [259,408), [439,552). These measurements are training support, not acceptance thresholds.

At demo10300 280 the red cube center is z=0.04574 m, the gripper command is -1, and fingers are about 0.01828/0.01824 m. Containment is already true while the payload is likely held. At 285 this remains so. At 290 the command is +1, fingers are about 0.03707 m, red has settled to z=0.036 m, and TCP is still low at z=0.04528 m. At 320 the TCP has retreated to z=0.17754 m without moving red, and at 414 the empty/open hand approaches blue at z=0.26767 m. Blue repeats the distinction: at 655 it is contained but closed-held; at 660 the first observed open command still has closed fingers; at 665 fingers are about 0.03822 m and blue is settled; by 720/741 TCP is near z=0.3165 m. Demo10307 641/646 versus 650/659/680 independently supports command lag, partial opening, settling, and decoupled retreat. Finger qvel alone is misleading: significant opposing finger velocities coexist with nearly constant closed grasp widths.

The useful entry information is causal qpos aperture, qvel, both object poses and goals, TCP/object proximity and two-frame changes, and which object remains unplaced. A held red object must continue lower/open even when its goal is true and blue remains pending. An incoming open approach must be able to close/lift rather than assume a delivery invocation always starts attached. The learned per-object branches and inferred roles provide that distinction. The model learns to continue through a shared transition rather than treating containment as an automatic mode switch.

## Necessary interface adaptations

The research heuristic proposed eight causal frames, past actions, an intended/pending role interface, persistent filtering, and 1-2 executed actions around release. The fixed executable interface supplies exactly two raw observation frames, no past-action inputs or external role tokens, and executes eight actions per invocation. This implementation does **not** claim those unavailable capabilities.

A GRUCell is unrolled over the two available observations from a trainable initial state on every call. A learned probabilistic phase update mixes the previous phase distribution with a current emission. It provides short-window persistence, not a durable cross-call latch. There is no mutable hidden buffer that could accidentally advance during 100 DDPM reverse steps. Training replaces the older frame with the current frame with probability 0.15 to train a missing-history approximation. Deployment can repeat the current frame at a cold start. No timestamp, trajectory identifier, segment index or future state enters forward.

External intended/pending roles are replaced with **learned inferred** red/blue/none roles, weakly supervised by current geometry. Likely attached local work has priority over goal flags; remaining uncontained objects supply the pending role. These are not an external command interface and cannot resolve a caller's arbitrary intent in an otherwise identical observed state. Learned short-history belief can still be uncertain at a stationary held goal. Exact first-open timing may not be identifiable without more history or past commands.

The optional `model.beliefs(raw_history)` method provides diagnostic probabilities plus the current exact geometric per-object contract truth. The mandatory forward only returns predicted noise. There is no automatic framework exposure of diagnostic heads, no automatic stopping, and no model-controlled execution-length override. A caller using only the standard API must inspect causal observations and HANDOFF.json; one cannot assume it receives the diagnostic tensors.

## Learned architecture and representation

1. The original 47 raw state components are normalized by the supplied complete-demonstration mean and half-range. The same buffers are used everywhere. No per-skill or per-batch fit occurs. Actions remain the supplied normalized seven absolute Panda joint targets and gripper command.
2. Each frame has 118 features: normalized state (47), normalized causal increment (47, zero for the first frame), TCP/object, object/goal and TCP/goal displacements (18), and per-object containment margins (6). Relative spatial features use the supplied TCP world-axis half-ranges, not small fitted skill scales. Both object identities and absolute state remain available; this is not a claim of invariance or equivariant joint actions.
3. Rotated-cube XY extents are computed as 0.02 times the absolute row sums of the normalized wxyz quaternion rotation matrix. For a cube this equals the extrema of all eight rotated corners. XY margins use goal half-width 0.06 m; center-z margin uses 0.011 m. Margins are causal features and geometric labels, not a constant-only loss.
4. An MLP embeds each frame to 160 dimensions; a 192-dimensional GRUCell encodes the two frames. Per-object five-state distributions are updated by learned sigmoid interpolation between a prior distribution and new softmax emissions. States are acquisition/idle, likely attached, opening, released/settling near TCP, and placed/separated. The last state does not imply the hand is empty if it is already handling the other block.
5. Trainable heads predict per-object attachment, per-object containment, per-object disengagement plus global empty-hand readiness, inferred intended and pending roles, 15 future release hazards, and 16-step TCP/object/finger displacements. Phase probabilities and all classification/hazard probabilities, together with recurrent, frame and normalized raw skip features, form a 256-dimensional diffusion condition. Forecast outputs supervise the common encoder but are not themselves fed to the denoiser.
6. `appl.public.DiffusionBackbone` uses the fixed conditional U-Net widths 128/256/512, kernel 5, groups 8 and timestep embedding 128. It predicts epsilon for all 16 slots of the original action representation. Sampling, clipping, normalization inversion, optimizer, EMA and DDPM schedule are left to the supplied framework. No action mixing, controller, IK, FK, image encoder, collision guarantee or output phase clamp is implemented.

## Losses, labels and gradient paths

All pseudo-label construction uses `no_grad`; **all losses compare trainable predictions** or reconstructed clean actions with those targets. Labels and masks alone are not counted as priors. The standard masked epsilon loss remains coefficient 1 and covers all valid assigned actions, including both overlaps.

- **Phase NLL (0.10):** on two-frame predicted per-object distributions. Geometry/aperture pseudo-labels distinguish local closed grasp (mean finger <0.024 m, TCP distance <0.045 m), widening in a placement area (increment >0.0005 m and mean <0.0375 m), open contained settling near TCP, and contained separated objects. These are contact proxies, not contact sensor truth. Ambiguous intermediate attachment widths are excluded from attachment BCE, while phase labels remain weak categorical approximations.
- **Attachment BCE (0.05):** local closed objects are positive; far objects or wide fingers are negative; intermediate ambiguous cases are masked. This does not prove a grasp at a closed stationary source.
- **Containment BCE (0.05):** predicts the current rotated-cube containment predicates independently from disengagement. Exact truth is also separately computable for caller diagnostics. A geometric label with a trainable BCE head has a real gradient; a geometry-only penalty would not.
- **Readiness BCE (0.08):** predicts two disengagement probabilities and one global empty-hand readiness probability. Per-object positives require current containment, center settling within 0.003 m, current separation or mean fingers >0.035 m, and future object persistence within 0.004 m. Near-TCP positives additionally require a future TCP displacement >0.003 m while that object remains persistent, demonstrating decoupling. Future opening and settling are also directly learned by the displacement forecast below. Definite negatives include noncontainment, unsettled objects, likely attachment, or observed future loss of persistence. Other cases are masked, as are windows without at least three valid strictly future observations. Global empty-hand positives require wide fingers, no likely current attachment and persistence of any currently contained objects. It can be true during an empty initial approach with neither goal complete. This head is not a task-success predicate. These millimetre-scale numbers are **label-design hypotheses**, not refitted normalization constants, runtime waiting conditions or contract requirements.
- **Brier terms (0.02):** squared errors of predicted containment and readiness probabilities against the same masked labels. They encourage calibration but no calibration claim is made without evaluation.
- **Role CE (0.025 times the average of intended/pending CE):** preserves held-object priority in weak labels even if its goal is true. Inference receives predicted probabilities, never teacher roles.
- **Release-event survival likelihood (0.03):** causal belief predicts conditional open-command hazards at action slots 1 through 15. A positive event is the first demonstrated -/closed to +/open transition. At-risk labels require current likely attachment and the preceding command closed. Valid actions contribute negative log likelihood through the first event or to the end of a right-censored window. Already-open states are not incorrectly assigned a fresh release event. Native action history is used only for training targets.
- **Displacement forecast smooth L1 (0.10):** predicts changes of TCP XYZ, red XYZ, blue XYZ and both fingers over all 16 future-observation slots, normalized with the corresponding global observation half-ranges. This learns finger expansion, object settling and TCP withdrawal rather than supervising readiness only from current poses. **Relative-motion forecast (0.05)** explicitly compares predicted object-minus-TCP displacement with future training labels, using shared TCP-axis scales. Gradients flow to the forecast head and recurrent encoder.
- **Belief persistence (0.02):** squared difference between successive trainable phase probabilities only where pseudo-label phase is unchanged. The learned update gate, not a fixed wait, controls persistence. There is no prohibition on transitions or hard latch.
- **Detached-branch no-reclose (0.05):** reconstructs x0 from noisy actions and predicted epsilon. On demonstrated open, sufficiently open-finger, non-held branches with at least one contained object, penalizes `relu(0.8 - x0_gripper)^2`, multiplied by alpha_bar and restricted to alpha_bar >0.1. Native gripper normalization is exactly [-1,1]. Future/action masks are both applied. Demonstrated closing slots, including next acquisition, are excluded. This gives a direct gradient to the denoiser against reopening chatter/re-closing after release without overwriting sampled actions or globally requiring +1.

The action alignment is explicit: slot 0 is action t-1 and its future observation is the current state at t; slots 1..15 have strictly future after-action states. Readiness counts only slots 1 onward as future evidence. No future crosses a slice: all future losses, hazard likelihood and reconstructed-action penalties are masked. High-noise amplification is limited in the only x0 auxiliary by alpha weighting and an alpha cutoff. The other belief/forecast losses act on causal encoder predictions and are independent of DDPM noise amplitude.

## Completion, continuation and limitations

Formal task success is exactly red_at_goal AND blue_at_goal with full rotated XY containment and center-z tolerance 0.011 m. It does not require release, TCP clearance, zero velocity, or a sustained hold. The caller may stop at this success even with a held block. It must not describe that state as an empty-hand acquisition handoff. If reusable manipulation is needed, continue the learned opening/settling and retreat, and transfer to an acquisition policy that supports the actual transition state. A successor taking over early may receive held red at z roughly 0.084-0.169 m, not an empty hand; it must finish lower/open before pursuing blue.

All thresholds above are supervisory approximations from successful demonstrations. Stiction, missed grasps, bounce-out, unusual release latency and regrasp recoveries are not represented. A two-frame model may confuse stationary pre-open holds; an eight-action execution chunk may be too coarse near release. Readiness can be conservatively delayed or spuriously optimistic, and weak role inference can latch the wrong object. World-state features do not guarantee collision avoidance or generalization to new tray layouts. There is no guarantee of object preservation outside demonstrated support.

Dependencies are Torch and appl.public only. The fixed budget is 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW 1e-4/1e-6, EMA .999, cosine schedule with 500 warmup steps, DDPM 100 train/inference steps and last EMA selection, seed 0. No rollout success evaluation or performance-based model selection is available during implementation. Useful falsification tests would compare a memoryless DP, a short-history recurrent DP without separate readiness supervision, and this policy at randomized overlap entry times and perturbed release latency, measuring post-release preservation and unnecessary dwell separately from contract success. Passing the package checks is not evidence of those benefits.

# Stick-push sole N5 feedback decision

Decision: `no_revision`. This is an autonomous evidence-based decision by the fresh isolated Codex agent `/root/feedback_stick`; it does not choose the final deployed candidate or claim that further improvement is impossible.

The authorized packet contains four N5 systems, each evaluated on the same 10 IID, 20 compositional and 20 extrapolation episodes. B0 succeeded on 45/50 (15/20 E), P1 on 50/50, P2 on 49/50 (19/20 E), and P3 on 50/50. P1 and P3 both succeeded on every retained failure episode of B0 or P2. These observations support the adequacy of the existing world-relation candidates on this development sample. They do not establish a causal benefit from particular features, a reliable P1/P3 ordering, or transfer to N20, another seed, or locked test episodes.

I viewed all eight real feedback frames and the training-curve plot with the image tool. Each model's frames depict the same single successful compositional episode at step 50 and its terminal step (75 for B0/P1/P2, 74 for P3), despite the second filenames requesting step 200. The hand/tool/container arrangements and later container movement look similar across models. These frames do not show any retained failure, the initial approach, or enough detail to establish contact forces or secure grasp.

I inspected all four full 201-point training curves, including every reported numeric field and P3 auxiliary loss, and all four 50-record offline diagnostic files. The curves show large early loss reductions and low late reported losses with transient spikes; they do not identify a persistent optimization failure requiring a schedule change. P3's total includes its additional motion objective and is not directly comparable to the four-channel action losses. Fixed 20,000-update budgets remain appropriate to the unchanged experiment.

B0's five failed extrapolation episodes have recorded peak tool lifts of 0.00134–0.01815 m, and P2's one failed episode has 0.01117 m lift; successful episodes have much larger recorded lifts. This is descriptive evidence of different motion, consistent with an acquisition problem but insufficient for a causal grasp/contact diagnosis. The retained 5 cm proximity threshold is especially unsuitable for such a conclusion: 23 B0, 21 P1, 24 P2, and 22 P3 successful episodes never cross it. Observed goal-point distance is also not a success rule: a B0 failed episode reaches about 0.00306 m minimum distance, while successful terminal distances commonly remain about 0.11–0.12 m. Neither diagnostic becomes a model input, control target, phase label, or reward substitute.

I considered changing P2 back to a world frame, adding an acquisition-specific auxiliary objective, and reducing or removing P3's motion objective. The first largely recreates the already available P1; the second lacks a supported target or failure localization; the third lacks observed P3 failures or controlled evidence that its auxiliary objective is harmful. A new combination or fitted threshold would therefore be speculative tuning without a clearly supported mechanism. No substantive P4 is proposed, and no implementation or configuration is created.

All 29 packet-bound SHA256 values were independently verified. The record includes all 35 accessed-file hashes and distinguishes the 24 content/view accesses from 11 hash-only provenance accesses. Exact bound complete files, trajectory files, the task manifest and two rule files were hashed without parsing their contents. No implementation, other task, other N result/data, aggregate selection report, expert/reward source or locked test content was inspected. This consumes one feedback cycle using only the existing 200 scored development episodes, eight frames and plot; it creates no rollout, GPU work, optimizer update or further feedback cycle.

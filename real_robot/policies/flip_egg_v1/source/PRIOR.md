# Prior and implementation record

## Preservation and scope
This is an implementation of the existing Flip egg cut_v1, not another cut-stage experiment. `ORIGINAL_CUT.json` transcribes every original interval and split. Preparation writes `original_source_plan.json` verbatim from the supplied source plan, plus original recording metadata; the framework retains the original assignment, immutable source arrays/media and every authored version. The source plan's two responsibility groups and intervention exclusions are unchanged. The 20 executor prefixes overlap the full episodes and are deduplicated in the cache, not counted as new experience. Only the authorized Flip egg catalog is used; the wider authorization mentioning Push does not add Push recordings to this package.

## Evidence and residual learning problem
Inspected first and last rows of every full interval, every handoff and control end, and the actual third-view scenes across all twenty episodes. Initial gripper widths are about .138m, held widths about .0335/.0340m. Finite feedback alone is not grip force. Tool is a black slotted spatula, initially at a small dish. Visible variation includes pan position/handle heading, tool rest, egg extent/face appearance and prolonged insertion readjustment. 6200101: 1400/1700 and 6295601: 1400/1800 show readjustments. 6553801:1550/1580/1610 shows supported, airborne/tilted, then pan-resting egg. A hardcoded 180-degree wrist action is not justified. 6320101:2650, 6411901:2140, 6463201:2360 show human interventions; their earlier supplied supervision caps remain. No verified flip success labels exist, including for the apparently settled final frames.

The Agent selects the same-pan turn-over goal, retains its initial reference, observes retention/support, arbitrates interruption and verifies the outcome. The supplied planner/tracker solves arm kinematics and explicit geometry, not insertion/support/release strategy. That residual task-dependent choice needs all six local motion coordinates and recent two-view observations. One policy shares approach, insertion corrections, lifting, release-producing motion and immediate clearance. Pickup is not learned from prefixes. A new learned stop classifier would manufacture labels from recording endings, so none is implemented.

## Evidence-based representation adaptation
The starting prior required a measured blade template, six annotated landmarks, masks, measured pan plane and causal attachment estimates. None are supplied. Calibration is about 3.63mm pose consistency / 4.56px reprojection RMS, insufficient to invent a blade origin from pictures. Raw depth lacks validated scale/registration; the calibration board is not the pan surface. Recorded EE TCP [0,0,.24] and yaw -90 degrees is not the leading edge of the spatula. We therefore do NOT fill the missing quantities with estimates posing as measurements, or label occluded/airborne egg centroids as lying on a plane.

Instead, preserve the six-coordinate short-motion prior in the **measured recorded EE frame**. For a rigid retained known tool this is an alternative complete coordinate parameterization of robot-controlled tool motion, but it lacks an explicit tip lever arm and metric pan-relative invariance. Executor can directly interpret it without knowing a blade landmark origin. RGB contains the remaining egg/tool/pan relation and learned CNN features supply that information jointly with the actor. `scene_geometry_aux` is replaced, not left as an untrained runtime dependency: this implementation does not request or emit masks, landmarks, PnP, pan poses or a geometry success label. The visual encoder is fully trainable from the same motion loss, with no uninstalled external asset. It is not a semantic geometry service for the executor. Physical collision geometry and grasp/retention commissioning are still required separately.

This tradeoff recovers supervised interaction evidence from all episodes without fictitious labels and permits training now. It narrows the useful interface to known-setup held-tool proposals. It does not complete autonomous geometric acquisition or safe contact commissioning. Major camera changes, unknown grasp offsets, novel tools/foods/heat, clutter and dropped/off-pan recovery remain outside scope.

## Learning design
Two RGB views, aspect-preserving fixed crop/letterbox 3x144x192, five past/current observations spanning about .4s, plus the initial third-view scene. The reference is row0, never a final state or inferred landing goal. Scene selection is unambiguous only for this single-egg setup. Reference is useful for appearance and goal identity; outcome recognition remains Agent-owned, not established by the training loss. State features are EE position/rotation, tau_ext7 (joint torque estimates, not a Cartesian wrench), measured width, image age/validity, actual past dt and achieved past local rate. q/dq and gripper command are omitted: arm kinematics are executor-owned, and the measured dq is not a command. Position retained because camera is fixed and table height/arm reach matter; no unearned translation invariance claimed.

The small shared spatial CNN (16/32/48/64), 64-feature per view, 128-unit finite-history GRUCell and three diagonal-Gaussian modes follow the source prior. All weights train from scratch. Mode maximum, not mean of incompatible directions, is the proposal. Predictive spread includes within/between-mode variance but is not a calibrated epistemic/OOD certificate. Diffusion would add iterative sampling complexity for a single six-vector with little independent experience; no need demonstrated here. A finite rolling history replaces long TBPTT, making reset/resume semantics exactly reproducible and eliminating an episode clock.

Loss: normalized mixture NLL plus .2 responsibility-weighted smooth-L1 rate error (equivalent to scaled 0.1s endpoint error). Fixed scales .1m/s and .5rad/s. Log standard deviation bounded; no fake blade-axis regularizer. AdamW lr3e-4 wd1e-4, 100-step warmup, cosine decay to 15%, gradient norm1, batch24, seed1937, at most2000 updates. Equal-episode sampling; at most floor(.1B) static examples, roughly one third of other draws high-angular-motion. Never discard adjustment-heavy episodes. Mild consistent photometric augmentation, explicit camera dropout and past-history truncation only; no image yaw masquerading as lifted 3D geometry and no physics speed augmentation.

## Evaluation and readiness
Original episode blocks train 11, validation4, test5. Strict near-duplicate audit on 32 deterministic paired-image+pose probes per episode groups any confirmed cross-episode duplicates into the most restricted partition; it is not exhaustive semantic deduplication. Shared objects are intentional. Select by equal-episode validation loss at each100 steps, early stop after600 updates and8 nonimproving evaluations. Evaluate <=128 fixed anchors per episode. Test is measured once at stopping on a copy of the selected checkpoint, not used for selection. Report physical-unit rate MAE, zero-motion baselines, static/translation/rotation strata, within-episode progress quartiles and predictive spread coverage. Progress quartiles are evaluation-only, not online phase inputs. Leave-layout-out *retraining* is not done; do not claim it.

Preparation artifacts contain actual per-episode counts, rejected anchor reasons, target distributions, numerical reconstruction tests and RGB montages. Actual readiness is assessed against that manifest at submission, not assumed from potential counts. Training/interface measurements will be emitted separately by the framework. None establish physical robot success or safe deployment.

# Prior document: red_top_regrasp_lift__h03

## Assigned research hypothesis

The assigned heuristic is the **attachment-switch prior** for the `red_top_regrasp_lift` skill.  The hypothesis is that this slice contains an important ambiguity: the robot must approach and close on the red block, but it should begin lifting and hand off to red transport only after a stable grasp is likely.  The original heuristic proposes a latent attachment variable inferred from causal robot and object state, with training labels derived from future red-block displacement, finger closure, and stability of the TCP-red offset.

The assigned M1_v2 segment is the full expanded interval `[240, 530)` for each demonstration.  This includes the earlier shared transition from the drawer-open/pull skill, the reorientation and top-grasp approach, the close/lift event, and the later shared transition to red transport.  I keep that complete slice rather than training only on the narrow top-grasp indices.

## Executable adaptation

The available interface provides two causal state observations and fixed-horizon action diffusion.  There is no force sensing, tactile sensing, IK solver, image encoder, or external geometry module.  Therefore I implement attachment as a learned state-latent, not as a physical contact detector:

- The model reads only `raw_history [B, 2, 47]` at inference.
- A trainable encoder consumes the two normalized full observations plus relative features such as TCP-red position, two-frame TCP/red motion, finger aperture, drawer position, and red-to-goal vector.
- The encoder predicts three mode logits: `free_or_approach`, `closed_contact`, and `carried_attachment`, plus an attachment logit, a future-lift scalar, and a relative-offset prediction.
- The soft mode probabilities and attachment estimate condition a standard learned DDPM action denoiser.

Training may use `future_obs` only to create supervision targets for the latent heads.  Deployment never receives future observations.

## Architecture

`policy.py` builds `AttachmentSwitchDiffusionPolicy`:

1. **Shared-normalized observation features.**  The complete two-step 47-D state history is normalized with `appl.public.normalize_observation`, using the single M1_v2 normalizer fitted on complete original demonstrations.  I do not fit any tiny per-skill scale.  Additional relative features use broad fixed physical scales (for example 0.15 m for TCP-red position), not segment-specific statistics.
2. **Causal mode encoder.**  A small MLP with LayerNorm and SiLU activations maps 130 features to a hidden state.
3. **Attachment/mode heads.**  Trainable heads predict free/contact/carry logits, binary attachment, predicted future red lift, and TCP-red offset.
4. **Latent-conditioned diffusion decoder.**  The soft mode distribution is combined with learned mode embeddings and projected to a 320-D condition vector.  `appl.public.DiffusionBackbone` then predicts DDPM epsilon for the 16-step, 8-D normalized action sequence.

The policy is still a learned action diffusion model: all actions are generated by the DDPM denoising network, not by a scripted gripper or Cartesian controller.

## Losses and gradient paths

`compute_loss` returns the required `loss`, `diffusion_loss`, and `prior_loss`.

- `diffusion_loss` is the standard masked epsilon prediction loss against the sampled DDPM noise.
- `mode_loss` supervises the free/contact/carry head with soft labels.  The carried/attachment label is high when the current fingers are closed, the current TCP-red distance is small, the future red block lifts, and the future TCP-red offset remains stable.  The contact label covers closed/proximal states before reliable lift; the free label covers open approach and reorientation.
- `attach_loss` is binary BCE for the attachment logit using the same future-derived attachment score.
- `lift_loss` trains a scalar prediction of maximum future red z displacement.
- `rel_loss` trains a relative TCP-red offset prediction, weighted more strongly when the learned labels indicate closed contact or carry.
- A low-weight `gripper_consistency` term computes the denoised clean action estimate from the predicted epsilon and encourages open gripper commands in free mode and closed commands in contact/carry mode.  It is gated by alpha-bar to reduce high-noise x0 amplification.

All prior losses depend on trainable predictions.  None of them is a constant penalty computed only from observed poses.

## Causal handoff information

The policy can take over partway through a transition because the two observation frames expose the relevant state:

- `qpos[7:9]` gives current finger aperture and symmetry.
- `tcp_pose[:3]` and `red_pose[:3]` give the TCP-red offset in world metres.
- The two-frame change in TCP and red positions indicates whether the red block is static, being contacted, or co-moving with the hand.
- `drawer_position` indicates that the drawer-open precondition is already satisfied in this expanded slice.

In the early overlap the learned diffusion model covers retreat/reorientation from the drawer-pull configuration toward the top-grasp configuration.  In the later overlap it covers finger closure and lift.  A successor should take over when the causal observations show closed fingers, stable TCP-red offset, and red height around 0.30 m, not merely when the red block is geometrically near the hand at table height.

## Applicability and limitations

This prior is intended for the demonstrated state-based tabletop setting after drawer opening, with the red block observable.  It assumes the red pose, TCP pose, joint state, and gripper aperture are reliable.  It does not implement IK, collision constraints, object containment checks, image processing, or force-confirmed grasp detection.  If the robot pinches the wrong location, if the red pose estimate is wrong, or if contact dynamics differ significantly from the demonstrations, the learned attachment latent may be overconfident or fail to switch to carry mode.

The task completion definition remains the supplied three-goal rule: drawer open, red on the outside pad, and blue inside the drawer at the same observation.  The lift/attachment cues described here are skill handoff guidance only and do not redefine task success.

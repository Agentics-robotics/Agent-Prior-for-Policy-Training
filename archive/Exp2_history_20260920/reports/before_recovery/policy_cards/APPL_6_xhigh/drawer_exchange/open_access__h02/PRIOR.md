# Contact-event diffusion prior: open_access__h02

## Original hypothesis and assigned scope

The assigned heuristic statement is: **"Contact-event memory rather than episode clock: an ordered latent should disambiguate similar pre-contact, attached and released poses, improving gripper timing and takeover consistency."** The original heuristic is not modified. This document describes a particular executable adaptation, not additional results or a replacement heuristic.

The intended subgoal is to open the drawer beyond 0.26 m, relinquish the handle without losing the opening, and establish red pickup through initial lift. The assigned expanded slices are retained in full: actions [0,475) in most demonstrations, [0,476) in demo1001, and [0,474) in demo1005/1007/1008. The overlap with `evacuate_red` begins at original action 195 and extends through the end of each slice: 279-281 shared actions. No prefix-only training, action cropping, phase-based sampling, per-skill renormalization, replay, or script that chooses actions is implemented. All phases receive the ordinary masked action diffusion objective.

## Important interface adaptation

The research suggestion calls for approximately 0.5-1 seconds of causal recurrent observations and previous available commands. The fixed interface supplies **two observations at 20 Hz**, no explicit history-validity mask, no previous-command channel and no persistent recurrent-state argument. The implementation therefore cannot faithfully retain an externally observed half-second event history. It uses a GRU over the supplied two-frame window, reinitializes from that window on every invocation, and represents additional duration uncertainty with learned age-bin beliefs. Those beliefs are inferred latent variables, not a measured event clock. No mutable cache is kept across DDPM calls or option invocations; this avoids training/deployment mismatch and hidden state leakage between batch examples.

There are 16 duration bins at 0.05-second increments, with the final bin censored at 0.75 seconds or longer. At the oldest observation, a learned head initializes a distribution over every phase and every age, permitting takeover in the middle of any demonstrated phase. Only one actual within-window state transition is observed. Age beliefs additionally learn from masked future phase-label forecasts; age itself has no measured supervision and is not identifiable or calibrated as true elapsed time. It is a duration-augmented semi-Markov approximation, not evidence that full long-memory contact inference has been implemented. There is no episode timestep input, segment index, demonstration identifier, or prescribed skill schedule. DDPM's noise timestep and future chunk slot index are not episode clocks.

Exact duplicate input frames are treated as an uninformative second observation, suppressing the second recurrent/filter update. This is a practical padding approximation, not an actual supplied mask; it also treats exactly repeated real states as duplicates. During training, 15% of examples replace the oldest frame with the current frame. This trains truncated-history takeover without fabricating a previous action. Initial entry should provide the framework's two causal observations, duplicating the first state when nothing older is available, rather than treating an all-zero pose as valid history.

## Learned representation and denoiser

1. **Shared normalization.** Observation centering/scaling buffers come directly from `spec['normalizer']`, the common limits normalizer fitted on complete original demonstrations. Here `std` is the half-range, not a per-skill standard deviation. Quaternion components use the provided unit bounds. Normalized actions are the supplied affine encoding of seven absolute Panda joint targets plus gripper command. No scales are refitted or estimated from the expanded slice.
2. **Causal tokens.** Each token contains 47 shared-normalized observation entries, 47 normalized differences (zero at the oldest frame), nine relative-position coordinates, and one distinct-second-observation flag. Relative features are red-minus-TCP, blue-minus-TCP, and drawer-center-minus-TCP. Drawer center is [0.19-d,0,0.035]. Red/blue relative coordinates use root-sum-square shared coordinate scales. For drawer-relative coordinates, each coordinate uses the root-sum-square of its shared TCP scale and the shared drawer displacement scale as a conservative common length contribution; these are not newly fitted limits. Absolute poses remain available. These features do not make absolute joint actions equivariant.
3. **Recurrent contact representation.** A 128-wide token MLP and GRUCell produce the two causal hidden states. Learned emissions cover: handle approach, handle closure/readiness, handle pull, handle release, released retreat, red approach, red closure/readiness, and red initial lift. Readiness and closure are intentionally soft categories, not binary force/contact states.
4. **Ordered duration filter.** The oldest hidden state initializes a soft phase posterior and conditional age distributions. At the second observation, learned context-dependent hazards and a trainable phase-by-age bias permit staying in a phase while incrementing/censoring age, or advancing by exactly one phase while resetting age. The final phase is absorbing within the filter. Current learned emissions update this predicted joint distribution. There is no hard phase selection. Across policy calls every phase is reinitialized as possible, so the topology is a local bias rather than a global irreversible automaton. Initial hazard biases favor persistence, but duration hazards are trainable and no exact dwell time is enforced.
5. **Phase-specific conditioning adapters.** The current recurrent representation passes through eight separate 128-to-32-to-64 adapters, softly mixed by the filtered posterior. A separate 256-wide context path retains current geometry, differences and hidden-state change. Their concatenation with eight phase probabilities and eight phase-weighted normalized age expectations gives a 336-dimensional condition. This condition drives the shared U-Net's learned FiLM layers. The adapters are conditioning adapters, not eight independent U-Nets or eight action controllers. There is a direct geometric/proprioceptive conditioning path so uncertain phase inference does not discard useful state information.
6. **Real action diffusion.** The public Conditional U-Net, widths 128/256/512, predicts epsilon for [B,16,8] noisy normalized action chunks. Deployment `forward` uses only noisy action, noise timestep and raw causal history and returns epsilon of the same shape. The fixed 100-step DDPM sampler and shared decoder produce native absolute joint/gripper actions. The framework executes eight-action prefixes before replanning. The model does not implement an adaptive execution prefix, IK, action replay, a gripper override, or an external controller. Standard sampler clipping is not a proof of physical safety.

## Proxy supervision and exact losses

The loss is

`L = L_epsilon + 0.12 L_phase + 0.05 L_forecast + 0.02 L_dwell + 0.01 L_transition`.

**Noise loss:** `L_epsilon` is the public masked mean squared epsilon error over all valid action slots and all eight action dimensions. No action target is replaced with a heuristic controller output.

**Soft phase labels:** a training-only, no-gradient function produces eight overlapping phase probabilities from physical observations. It uses mean per-finger width; aperture changes or mean finger velocity; drawer displacement/velocity; TCP distance from a demonstrated handle-contact TCP locus [-0.140-d,0,0.128]; TCP/red distance and lateral alignment; red height; and valid one-step future red/TCP vertical co-motion. The locus is an observed contact-pose approximation, not an instrumented handle pose or an action goal. Label tolerances are soft physical lengths/speeds, not feature normalization fits. Co-lift evidence compares red/TCP velocity vectors and requires upward motion. Red proximity prevents confusing red's drawer-carried translation with a TCP grasp. A high red-height proxy can also support lift, so neither geometry nor width guarantees attachment. Targets receive 1% uniform smoothing. No discrete source time boundary is used for phase labels.

`L_phase` is soft cross entropy against learned phase probabilities, weighted 0.25 for the oldest and 0.75 for the current frame. Future changes can label a current transition, but never enter the encoder. Consequently the target can remain ambiguous for causally identical pre-event states; this implementation cannot reconstruct unavailable command timing.

**Future phase forecast:** a learned head takes the current recurrent representation, filtered phase posterior and censored dwell statistic, and predicts phase-hazard offsets for the next 15 chunk slots. Repeated stay/advance transitions propagate the learned joint phase-age distribution without access to future observations. Slot zero is the current distribution; `L_forecast` is soft cross entropy against masked future phase labels. This trains duration/transition parameters using the available approximately 0.75-second supervision horizon; it does not feed a future pose to the action denoiser or generate actions itself. No autoregressive future observation encoder is used.

**Alignment and masks:** with current observation t, action slot j is t-1+j and `future_obs[j]` is t+j, so current co-motion labels use future slot 1, not slot 0. Future label sequences use the next valid future slot for motion; absent future motion contributes no co-lift evidence. All forecast losses use `future_mask`; all action losses use `mask`. Last-slot next-motion validity is zero. Thus endpoint padding does not invent a terminal attachment event, and no target crosses the assigned slice. Current observation phase supervision does not need a future label to remain defined.

**Dwell/anti-chatter:** `L_dwell` is Jensen-Shannon divergence between the two learned phase marginals. Its weight is the squared proxy stability score `(1 - TV(q_old,q_current))^2`, multiplied by the distinct-history flag. It is softened at actual proxy transitions rather than demanding an absolutely static phase at closure.

**Transition consistency:** `L_transition` is KL divergence from the filtered current learned phase marginal to the learned ordered one-step predicted marginal, averaged over distinct histories. This discourages emissions from repeatedly overriding the ordered prior. The topology itself implements the ordering; this is not a penalty on independent observed poses.

**Gradient paths:** noise loss trains the U-Net, context network, phase-specific adapters, GRU, emissions, initial-age beliefs and hazards through differentiable posterior conditioning. Phase and future cross entropies train the GRU and learned phase/duration heads; the future head is trained only as an auxiliary predictor. JS and KL regularizers differentiate through both learned distributions. No prior loss is a constant pose-only penalty. Auxiliary losses do not directly constrain the U-Net's reconstructed clean action or impose robot kinematics; their effect on actions is through shared learned conditioning and the diffusion objective. There is no high-noise x0 auxiliary amplification because no reconstructed-action auxiliary is used.

## Takeover, continuation and successor interface

Use the causal qpos[7:9], qvel, drawer d/vd, TCP/red/blue world poses, and their available changes. At initial rest the fingers are about 0.04 m each. In mid-pull the roughly 0.007 m fingers, moving drawer and handle-side TCP distinguish handle attachment from free travel. After release, opening fingers, increased TCP height and changing TCP/red offset identify retreat and approach. Near red, roughly 0.0182 m fingers with correlated vertical lift distinguish the later pickup state from open-finger approach at nearly identical TCP pose. TCP orientation and arm joint state remain available to distinguish free-space routes. Near-zero TCP speed alone is not a reliable event cue.

Do not terminate solely because d exceeds 0.26 m. This policy learns the complete shared interval: finish pulling, release, retreat, approach red, close, begin lift. Continuing it is supported while these phases remain consistent with the demonstration manifold. The API may transfer earlier in the overlap to a successor trained on that same contact evolution, or transfer at the supported late state with d about 0.300 m, closed last command, fingers about 0.0182 m each and red/TCP near z=0.143 m while rising. The endpoint is an initial lift, not completed red evacuation. Continued lifting far beyond this endpoint is outside this policy's assigned support.

Pass the actual causal observations and preserve the current physical gripper state. An external system may also retain available recent commands for a capable successor, but this model has no command argument and does not require a private latent at takeover. Do not forcibly reopen fingers at an option switch. `HANDOFF.json` records these cues and evidence. No claimed switch-timing evaluation has been performed.

Task success remains exactly the supplied completion contract: drawer open AND red on the outside pad AND blue inside the drawer at one observation, using the specified strict z/opening tests and rotated full-XY containment tests. Additional gripper release, low velocity, TCP clearance or sustained holding is not required. Initial red lift alone is not whole-task completion. No actions from the assigned expanded slice are omitted under that definition; later red transport and blue insertion belong to other slices.

## Applicability, limitations and scientific status

This is a state-based PyTorch model using only torch and the supplied `appl.public` numerical backbone/loss. No images, force sensors, FK/IK provider, external dynamics model, private files, or per-trajectory lookup tables are used. Optimizer, DDPM, safe execution and accounting remain framework-owned. The declared training budget is seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW 1e-4 with weight decay 1e-6, cosine schedule with 500-step warmup, max gradient norm 1, and last EMA at decay 0.999. No performance-driven selection or budget change is requested.

The prior predicts improved contact timing and timing-shifted takeover relative to memoryless or clock-conditioned alternatives; this design session supplies no comparative or rollout evidence for that prediction. Geometry and compliance remain demonstrated ones. The ordered topology can obstruct retries or reversed-phase recovery. Reinitialization avoids requiring a predecessor's private latent but weakens long-term consistency. Two-frame aliasing, inferred age uncertainty, aperture servo lag, duration saturation and fixed eight-action execution can still cause premature release, delayed closure or phase chatter. Latent posterior certainty on missed grasps or novel geometry is not calibrated. Failed-contact recovery, invariance, collision avoidance and hard attachment constraints are not implemented.

Useful future tests are switches throughout the measured overlap, deliberately truncated histories, changed dwell timing, and ablations of recurrence, age bins, dwell regularization and phase adapters against memoryless/clock-conditioned baselines. The interface check only validates executable training and DDPM/EMA reload behavior; it must not be interpreted as learned manipulation performance.

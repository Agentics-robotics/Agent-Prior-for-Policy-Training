## Necessary implementation adaptations

1. The provided batch exposes `future_obs[B,16,47]`, not an arbitrary complete future trajectory. Slot zero is the current state after the t-1 action; slots 1–15 are genuinely future. Therefore the high-level target is the **first detected semantic change in that available window, or the last valid future state if the event lies beyond it**. This is a local spatial intent hierarchy, not supervision of all distant maximum-height transitions. The duration coordinate is a capped lookahead in units of 15 control steps (at most about 0.75 s at 20 Hz), accompanied by a censor probability. Censored duration is a lower-bound window endpoint, not an estimate of the full remaining phase duration. Near slice ends, the window shortens and never crosses to another skill.
2. There are no annotated events or contacts. Training-only geometric proxies identify seven events: opening prerequisite, release, retreat-high, pickup-low, lift-high, target-high and place-low. The extra opening event covers the expanded prefix. Target-high also includes unloaded high approach. There are three selected entities: drawer frame, red and blue. The drawer center is a frame proxy, not a measured handle pose.
3. The action sampler has a stateless epsilon-forward interface. We implement a learned auxiliary waypoint VP diffusion denoiser and use a deterministic five-update central DDIM path to compute its intent from the current causal context. It starts at a zero central latent, uses alpha levels 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 0.95, then 1, and broadly clamps clean coordinates to [-5,5] for numerical stability. It is not a stochastic sample from the exact conditional waypoint distribution. The independently fixed **action** DDPM, its noising, 100 inference steps and clipping are unchanged. No intent is resampled randomly between action-denoising steps.
4. There is no persistent high-level event cache or implemented separate slower event clock. The same history yields the same predicted intent at all action DDPM steps. A new history refreshes the entire hierarchy at each replanning invocation; the framework normally executes 8 actions at 20 Hz before replanning. State and learned event probabilities, rather than elapsed duration, guide the updated intent. The API decides invocation duration and transfer. The model does not emit a compulsory private handoff code.

These adaptations preserve a learned spatial-intent-to-action hierarchy, but weaken claims about truly long-window planning and independent high-level temporal persistence. They must be considered in any comparison with a flat action diffusion model.

## Causal representation and architecture

Only two raw causal 47-dimensional observations enter `forward`. The shared complete-demonstration limits normalizer is used without refitting. qpos, qvel, TCP/object translations, drawer position/velocity and both goals are retained. Each quaternion is converted to the first two columns of its normalized rotation matrix (six dimensions), so the orientation **representation** is exactly invariant to quaternion sign. There is no claim of world-frame SE(3) invariance or equivariant absolute joint actions.

Each frame additionally contains six relative xyz vectors: TCP-red, TCP-blue, red-pad, blue-blue-goal, TCP-pad and TCP-drawer-center. Each axis is divided by the elementwise maximum of the shared TCP/red/blue translation half-ranges, rather than any narrow skill-specific scale. Frame width is 71. Concatenating previous frame, current frame and four times their difference gives 213 causal features, encoded by a 256-wide two-layer normalized MLP. Finger positions, velocities and object/TCP changes help distinguish approach from retreat, held motion from empty-hand motion, and release from grasp acquisition.

Learned heads predict seven event logits, three selected-entity logits and one duration-censor logit. Their probabilities and the causal context condition a 256-wide, three-residual-block epsilon denoiser for a 35-dimensional waypoint. It uses a continuous sinusoidal log-SNR embedding. The waypoint coordinates are:

- future TCP xyz relative to the selected entity's **current** center (3);
- future selected-entity xyz relative to its current goal (3);
- future TCP and selected-entity sign-invariant orientation representations (6+6);
- future seven arm qpos and two individual finger positions in shared normalization (9);
- future drawer position in shared normalization (1);
- future endpoint TCP and entity single-step displacement, each multiplied by eight and divided by the shared xyz scale (3+3);
- capped duration/lookahead coordinate (1).

The selected entity can be a drawer-center proxy with identity orientation and goal center [-0.11,0,0.035] m, corresponding to the demonstrated 0.30 m opening. Red and blue use their observed goal fields. These constants define label geometry, not joint actions. The displacement coordinates deliberately retain ongoing motion at the expanded endpoint; they are not a zero-velocity endpoint constraint.

The 35-coordinate predicted waypoint and 11 semantic probabilities feed a learned 256-dimensional intent embedding. A separate 128-dimensional current-state embedding is retained for local posture/feedback. The resulting 384-dimensional condition drives the standard conditional action U-Net (128/256/512 widths). Its sole action output is epsilon for the 16 by 8 noisy normalized joint/gripper action chunk. Original absolute joint-target semantics and shared action normalization are unchanged. The state bypass is useful for local feedback but means intent use is encouraged, not mathematically forced; a waypoint ablation is necessary.

## Label construction and losses

All future processing happens under `no_grad` in `targets`; `forward` never calls the labeler. Red-on-pad uses the supplied height interval and rotated cube XY extents. Other event thresholds are loose semantic annotation conventions, not deployment gates: a drawer-side TCP region identifies the prefix; total finger opening below 0.06 m plus TCP/object proximity (0.045–0.05 m) suggests holding; target proximity, heights near 0.27–0.34 m, and pad proximity separate lift/transfer/place/retreat. Red remains the selected entity during low retreat from the pad before blue becomes the next entity. These proxies can be ambiguous or wrong and do not certify collision-free reachability or physical grasp. A first change of either event or selected entity chooses a future target; otherwise the farthest valid state is used. Samples without any valid genuinely future slot contribute no auxiliary loss and use predicted intent for action training.

For each batch, the central waypoint is generated differentiably by the auxiliary denoiser. Half of examples randomly use the true waypoint plus independent 0.02-coordinate noise and true semantic labels to condition action denoising; the other half use exactly the predicted waypoint/semantics used at inference. The predicted branch is not detached. Teacher forcing is only for training and is explicitly not a causal deployment input.

The total loss is:

`L = L_action_epsilon + 0.4 L_waypoint_epsilon + 0.6 L_waypoint_Huber + 0.15 L_event_CE + 0.1 L_entity_CE + 0.05 L_censor_BCE`.

- Action epsilon MSE uses `batch.mask` and the fixed framework noise and action timesteps.
- Waypoint epsilon MSE independently noises the future-derived waypoint using the batch's VP alpha_bar (numerically bounded away from zero/one), and trains the high-level epsilon denoiser with current-state conditioning.
- Central-waypoint Huber loss compares the generated five-step intent to its future target. Per-coordinate weights are 1.5 for the six position coordinates, 0.25 for twelve orientation coordinates, 0.5 for seven arm coordinates, 1 for two finger coordinates and drawer position, and 0.5 for six displacement coordinates and capped duration. The sum is divided by the sum of these weights.
- Event/entity cross-entropies and censor BCE train the corresponding learned heads. Capped duration is part of both waypoint denoising and Huber supervision. It is not an independently enforced timing controller.

All auxiliary terms are averaged over valid future-supervised examples. Padded target candidates are excluded. The denoising loss trains the waypoint MLP and causal encoder; Huber gradients flow through the unrolled central denoising path; semantic gradients train their heads and encoder; predicted-intent action gradients also reach the entire hierarchy. No loss is merely an observed-pose penalty constant with respect to the model. No clean action reconstruction with amplified high-noise inverse-alpha factors is used for a geometric constraint. There is no learned or analytic FK/IK module.

## Applicability, continuation and completion

Use this skill in the demonstrated ordered route with comparable drawer, blocks and pad. It can take over during the moving-handle prefix, with an already-open elevated gripper, with red partially lifted, while red descends over the pad, during post-release retreat or during blue pickup. Use the two observation histories, qvel, drawer velocity, finger opening and TCP/object motion rather than duration alone to identify these cases. A geometric red subgoal can hold while retreat and blue acquisition are still unfinished. Transfer may be early in the shared suffix if the insertion policy explicitly supports finishing red placement, or later with blue already following the rising TCP and fingers still closed. Passing physical observations/history is sufficient; no private latent is required.

The global completion rule remains drawer position strictly greater than 0.26 m, full rotated-extent XY red containment on the pad with red center z in (0.014,0.031) m, and full rotated-extent XY blue containment in the drawer with blue center z in (0.053,0.074) m, at one observation. Release, TCP clearance, low velocity and a sustained hold are **not** additional task completion requirements. This skill's transition-readiness guidance does not override that contract. This expanded slice ends during blue lift and does not claim to learn the remaining insertion suffix.

## Dependencies, limitations and falsification

Dependencies are Torch, math and the public DiffusionBackbone/epsilon loss only. No data files, external assets, planner, IK, image encoder, replay or scripted action controller are loaded. All trainable modules and normalization buffers are in the model state for EMA/reload. Fixed training is 20,000 updates, batch 128, seed 0, AdamW 1e-4 with 1e-6 decay, 500-step warmup then cosine, gradient norm 1, EMA 0.999, last EMA selected. No performance-based tuning or checkpoint selection is requested.

Clearance is encouraged indirectly through demonstrated waypoint labels and actions, not by a collision inequality. Point poses omit drawer rim/contact geometry. Only one route family is supported; recovery after a failed grasp, blocked drawer, displaced pad or radically different object placement is untested. Thin semantic thresholds and future censoring limit event/duration fidelity. The deterministic auxiliary central path can average ambiguous waypoint alternatives. Two-frame motion and observed qvel may not resolve hidden contact history. Low-level state bypass can reduce bottleneck reliance. High-level lag, lateral low crossings, premature release/phase transfer, or chasing an object that did not follow are expected failure signatures. Appropriate tests compare flat action diffusion with identical observations/history, ablate waypoint supervision, evaluate switch times throughout both overlaps, and vary action horizon. No learned-policy success, invariance of joint actions, collision certification or failure recovery has been measured in this design session.

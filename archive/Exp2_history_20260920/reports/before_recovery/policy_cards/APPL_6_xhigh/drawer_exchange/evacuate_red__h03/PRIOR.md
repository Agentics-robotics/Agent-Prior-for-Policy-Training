# Prior: attachment-consistent action learning

## Identity and scope

Policy `evacuate_red__h03`, heuristic 3, skill `evacuate_red`, M1_v2.

The assigned research hypothesis is: **approximately conserve a held block's tool-relative transform, then switch that constraint off at release; masked rigidity should improve coordinated arm/gripper action chunks without imposing a nominal grasp offset.** The assigned source heuristic is unchanged. The choices below are this implementation's adaptations, not amendments to that heuristic or claims of measured improvement.

The policy trains on all twelve supplied expanded slices. Their starts are 195 and their exclusive stops are, in demonstration order 1000 through 1011, 845, 859, 847, 845, 846, 852, 846, 847, 853, 846, 849, 846. No transition phase or training example is intentionally discarded from diffusion imitation. Incoming overlap includes remaining handle pull, handle release, free retreat/approach, red finger closure and red lift. Outgoing overlap includes red descent, release, empty retreat, approach to blue, finger closure and initial blue lift. This is not a red-only transport policy with fixed red attachment at initialization.

## Causal representation and learned modules

All model code uses only Torch and the public diffusion backbone. No images, analytic forward kinematics, IK, simulator, external contact estimator or previous-command observation is available. Although the original heuristic suggests using available previous commands, this interface provides none at inference. Consequently they are omitted from the causal encoder. Demonstrated action commands are used only in the supervised rollout and training-mask construction.

1. The full 2 x 47 raw history is normalized by the exact shared normalizer in `spec`. Its `std` values are half-ranges, not sample standard deviations. No per-skill normalization is fitted, no raw observation is clipped, and absolute action encoding remains the framework's shared affine normalization.
2. For both observations, compute the red and blue tool-relative translations and unit wxyz orientations. Quaternions used in derived features are normalized and canonicalized using the sign of their largest-magnitude component, reducing the near-180-degree sign ambiguity. This does not claim equivariance of joint actions. Derived translations use one isotropic scale: the largest common positional half-range among the TCP and block XYZ fields, approximately 0.309 m. Quaternion components retain unit scales. Include the two derived transforms and their one-step differences.
3. Include causal handle coordinates [TCP-X + drawer position, TCP-Y, TCP-Z] at both observations and their difference. Handle-X scale is the root-sum-square of shared TCP-X and drawer scales; YZ use shared TCP scales. These are features, not a motion controller.
4. A 145-input MLP with widths 256 and 192 encodes these features. The full history retains qpos, qvel, drawer velocity, absolute poses, goals and finger states. A four-class learned posterior distinguishes none/handle/red/blue. A second head estimates two continuous relative poses through trainable residual refinements of the current measured transforms. This observed-transform skip avoids inventing an initial nominal grasp; the head learns the refinement/persistence estimate, not an unobserved contact transform from scratch.
5. The history embedding, posterior and posterior-weighted refined block transforms are projected to a 256-dimensional condition. The public conditional U-Net predicts epsilon for all 16 absolute joint/gripper action slots. Full-state features are never removed by attachment gating: free retreat and pregrasp motion remain representable.
6. A training-only action-conditioned GRUCell, hidden width 192, recursively predicts TCP/red/blue poses, drawer position and two finger positions. Its 24-state output uses a 128-wide MLP and shared-scale increments, with each pose quaternion normalized after each step. Its inputs are the current predicted state, current absolute encoded action and a relative chunk-slot coordinate. Its hidden state starts from the causal condition. No future teacher states are fed as inputs. Another head predicts a four-class attachment distribution from rollout hidden states.

The slot coordinate is relative within a sampled chunk, not a demonstration index or scripted task clock. Slot zero represents action t-1 whose result is current observation t, so that rollout state is copied from the current observation. Future state supervision and consistency use slots 1-15, after actions t through t+14. All sixteen slots retain the ordinary masked epsilon loss.

## Training labels, masks and executable adaptation

There is no contact ground truth. Attachment identity and constraint masks are explicitly **uncertain pseudo-labels** from consecutive observed poses, not an oracle. Current-posterior labels use only the two observed history frames. Future-posterior labels use consecutive target observations; the first pair is previous history observation to future slot zero. Those future frames never enter `forward` or its encoder.

For block evidence, smooth aperture gates prefer per-finger means between roughly 0.012 and 0.024 m, with 0.0015 m sigmoid transition scales. Evidence also requires tool/object proximity (0.045 m decay scale), relative translation agreement (0.004 m decay scale) and relative rotation agreement (0.12 rad decay scale). TCP and object displacement magnitudes corroborate co-motion, saturating at 0.0015 m per observation. Static near-object closed fingers yield only approximately 0.55 attachment evidence before other gates, leaving substantial probability on none. This avoids treating a closed command as contact proof. Soft scores for handle/red/blue and a none residual are normalized to a distribution.

Handle labels use narrow fingers, proximity to the observed handle region (TCP-X + drawer approximately -0.14 m, Y approximately zero, Z approximately 0.128 m), and observed X-coupling. The region has broad tolerances of 0.05/0.05/0.04 m and is only a training-label heuristic. This empirical handle evidence is not a calibrated handle-pose sensor. Handle consistency itself uses only TCP-X plus drawer position, never block rigidity or a handle rotation target.

Block rigidity is enabled only when block evidence exceeds 0.80 and co-motion exceeds one quarter of its saturation scale. Handle rigidity requires evidence above 0.75 and drawer displacement above 0.0005 m. Valid adjacent future masks and a negative demonstrated gripper command are additionally required. Opening is therefore not constrained, including the first opening command while fingers still appear closed. The uncertain soft identity labels also explicitly teach none on open-finger retreat. Masks depend on **labels**, not predicted posteriors; the network cannot escape the consistency loss by lowering its predicted attachment probability. These constants are physical pseudo-label tolerances, not refitted feature normalization, runtime rejection boundaries or action rules. Training still imitates all low-confidence and release actions through diffusion and state supervision.

This is an executable adaptation of the hypothesis: a learned dynamics surrogate replaces unavailable joint-to-object kinematics. The surrogate is trained on real action/future-state pairs and on denoised actions, and the rigidity cost acts on its predictions. There is no claimed hard physical constraint on the executed robot.

## Losses and gradient paths

Let `D` be the public valid-action masked epsilon MSE. Reconstruct the DDPM clean estimate from predicted epsilon using the supplied alpha_bar. For auxiliary rollout only, clip that estimate to [-1.5, 1.5] in encoded action coordinates. The actual sampler and its clipping/bounds are unchanged. Reconstructed-action auxiliary errors receive per-example weight alpha_bar squared. The reduction denominator counts valid targets rather than weighted targets, so high-noise downweighting is not canceled.

The rollout is evaluated twice, once with demonstrated encoded actions and once with reconstructed actions. Denote these GT and X0. State losses `S_GT` and `S_X0` combine shared-scale smooth-L1 on nine Cartesian position components, drawer position and two fingers, plus the mean sign-invariant squared SO(3) geodesic error of three poses divided by pi squared. Future and action validity masks apply; padded states are never supervised as real transitions.

`C` is current soft attachment cross-entropy plus one half future cross-entropy from the GT rollout and one half from the X0 rollout. X0 classification uses the same alpha_bar-squared weighting. It trains the causal identity encoder and the future release/attachment predictor.

`T` supervises the refined continuous block transforms against their current measured values, weighted by current block probabilities. It also supervises them against future slots 1-4 only where that same block remains plausible in all intervening pseudo-labels and the future target is valid. This is a training persistence target for a causal prediction, not future information passed to deployment.

`R_GT` and `R_X0` sum two terms: (a) confident-block masked adjacent predicted tool-relative translation change, squared and divided by the common relative scale, plus predicted relative-orientation squared geodesic change divided by pi squared; (b) confident-handle masked adjacent change in predicted TCP-X plus predicted drawer position, squared and divided by the shared handle-X scale. Each type is separately averaged over its valid active windows. No handle YZ or orientation rigidity is enforced. A zero-active-window term is finite and contributes zero.

The exact objective is:

`loss = D + 0.20 S_GT + 0.10 S_X0 + 0.05 C + 0.02 T + 0.05 R_GT + 0.10 R_X0`.

All reported prior terms are functions of trainable predictions. In particular, consistency compares **predicted** TCP/object/drawer states, not two observed poses alone. GT rollout supervision anchors the surrogate to measured consequences. X0 state, classification and rigidity losses backpropagate through the recurrent state predictor, through reconstructed action, into the epsilon denoiser; they also update the predictor and causal encoder. Causal posterior and transform heads receive their own supervised gradients and diffusion-conditioning gradients. Quaternion products, rotations and geodesic computations are differentiable with normalization safeguards. The learned rollout and action model are jointly optimized, not separately frozen or claimed to be a validated dynamics engine.

## Deployment, continuation and termination

Deployment `forward` receives only noisy actions, diffusion timestep and two causal raw observations. It recomputes the causal posterior and transforms, conditions the denoiser and returns predicted noise. The auxiliary rollout is not executed by `forward`; there is no online optimization, uncertainty-triggered prefix shortening, projection, constraint solver, scripted waypoint sequence, replay or external action controller. The framework supplies the fixed 100-step DDPM, action decoding/bounds, execution of eight-step prefixes, EMA and replanning. The final budget remains 20,000 updates, batch 128, seed 0, fixed optimizer and schedule, last EMA selection.

The observation history distinguishes supported partial transitions through aperture changes, absolute tool/target relations, object-versus-tool motion, and drawer opening/velocity. It therefore supports entering already handle-attached, in free motion, while closing on red, during red lift, or in the outgoing red-to-blue overlap without a private latent initialized by a predecessor. It cannot resolve all stationary-contact ambiguities or infer long hidden history from two observations.

Continue this policy to finish remaining opening, red placement/release, retreat or blue pickup if those transitions are still in progress and no suitable successor is selected. Transfer to an insertion policy at a supported early overlap state only if that successor covers red descent/release. A useful late handoff has red settled on the pad, fingers closed on blue with repeatable tool-relative pose, blue lifted to about 0.14 m and the drawer still open. Preserve blue attachment rather than injecting an opening command. Provide the physical history so the successor re-estimates this itself. These are semantic invocation cues, not internal rules executed by this model.

The complete task succeeds only according to the supplied contract: drawer position strictly greater than 0.26 m, red fully within the pad in XY accounting for rotated block extents and center z strictly between 0.014 and 0.031 m, and blue fully inside the drawer cavity in XY with center z strictly between 0.053 and 0.074 m, simultaneously. Release, TCP clearance, zero speed and sustained holding are **not** additional success requirements. This slice establishes blue initial lift rather than final insertion; it does not redefine the final endpoint or claim to cover the later insertion-only actions.

## Dependencies, limitations and falsifiable prediction

Dependencies are the shared range normalizer, the given state fields, Torch and the public conditional U-Net. The model assumes the observed small rigid-block grasp variations are informative for future chunks. The measured 9-10 mm world offset is evidence, not calibration or an invariant of arbitrary grasps. No invariance of absolute Panda joint targets is claimed.

Pseudo-labels can confuse support/pushing with attachment, especially during stationary closure. Their conservative moving masks omit some genuine held windows. Conversely, broad approach proximity alone is insufficient to guarantee contact. Losses are soft and local, the rollout can ignore some action information, and jointly trained dynamics can be inaccurate on generated actions despite supervision. Alpha weighting and clipping reduce but do not eliminate this risk. The policy has no demonstrated dropped-object recovery, regrasp, slip recovery, collision guarantee or tolerance to arbitrary handoff error. Finger-velocity asymmetry alone is deliberately not treated as slip. Full-state dependence means this is not a frame-invariant policy.

A testable expectation is reduced learned relative-transform drift and more coordinated arm/gripper chunks under supported grasps, while retaining legitimate release. A scientifically relevant ablation sets the two R weights to zero while retaining architecture, data, budget, classifier and supervised rollout. Compare lift/drop prediction, actual grasp retention and handoff behavior, and check for suppressed release. Interface validation is not evidence for any of those performance outcomes.

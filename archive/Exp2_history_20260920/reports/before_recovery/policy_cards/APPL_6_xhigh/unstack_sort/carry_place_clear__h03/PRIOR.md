# Contract-aware outcome prior for carry_place_clear__h03

## Assignment and scientific hypothesis

This package implements assigned heuristic 3 for `carry_place_clear`, without changing its segmentation or source heuristic. The source hypothesis is to optimize rotated object containment and preserve achieved goals, rather than insist on an exact TCP or pad-center trajectory. A contract-aware outcome prior might exploit placement tolerance while retaining release and retreat for useful handoffs. This is a hypothesis, not a measured policy improvement.

The task contract is world-frame full rotated XY containment of each cube in its own square pad, with cube half-size 0.020 m, pad half-size 0.060 m, and center-z error at most 0.011 m. Goals are red (-0.24, -0.25, 0.02) and blue (-0.24, 0.25, 0.02) m in the demonstrations; the model reads goals from observations rather than substituting these constants. Both colors must satisfy the contract. One actual satisfying observation suffices. Opening, clearance, zero velocity and sustained stability are NOT additional task-success predicates.

## Executable adaptation of the source proposal

The research proposal mentions persistent target/next tags, command history, and optional outcome-guided sampling. The actual forward interface supplies only two raw 47-dimensional observations and the noisy action/timestep. There are no tag, past-command, image or simulator-kinematics inputs. This implementation therefore conditions on BOTH color identities, both goals, current achieved-pad status, qpos/qvel and the TCP/object relative positions and motion. It infers the demonstrated active manipulation from those causal cues. The inference agent can choose a policy and invocation duration, but this package cannot accept a separate persistent target tag. Near an ambiguous motionless state, two observations cannot recover unobserved intent or elapsed dwell time. Current noisy action slots are denoising variables, not an observed command log.

The fixed sampler remains ordinary clipped epsilon-prediction DDPM. Instead of modifying the sampler or using gradients inside deployment forward, this package uses **training-time outcome-energy guidance amortized into the denoiser**. Causal progress predictions are also diffusion conditioning at training and inference. The outcome ensemble is trained and checkpointed, but not called to rerank samples at runtime. This avoids unsupported sampler hooks and inference autograd. Setting both goal-energy weights to zero gives the documented matched dynamics-auxiliary ablation; it is not a reported experiment.

## Representation and learned architecture

Actions remain the shared-normalized seven absolute Panda joint targets plus gripper command. The framework decodes them at 20 Hz. There is no scripted controller, action lookup, IK, FK, action-coordinate equivariance or hard Cartesian projection.

Every state and action uses the one supplied complete-original-data normalizer (normalizer identity `5445b8ee07ea31ca96be698930aa68aa28456023af51c213860aed01a97316fb`). Limits-mode `std` is a half-range. The model does not fit any scales. Drawer compatibility channels are omitted from its feature vector.

The 165-dimensional causal feature vector contains: two normalized 45-channel observations (90), their difference (45), each object's goal-relative position and TCP-relative position at both times (24), and current signed contract slacks for both objects (6). Goal-relative features use the corresponding shared object-position scales. TCP-relative features use the square root of the sum of the squared shared TCP and object scales. The extra slacks are divided by contract tolerances and saturated to [-5,5]; this is feature engineering, not clipping the globally normalized observations. Quaternion components use the prescribed unit bounds. Differences encode short-term motion, not long-term memory.

A 165->256->192 MLP with SiLU and final LayerNorm/SiLU produces a causal latent. A 192->128->64 MLP predicts four phase logits for each of 16 outcome slots: red contained, blue contained, both fingers at least 0.038 m, and open fingers with TCP z at least 0.250 m. These last two are manipulation-phase labels, not task completion conditions or verified release labels. Their sigmoid predictions, never ground-truth future phases, concatenate with the latent into 256-dimensional conditioning for the public conditional temporal U-Net (128/256/512 widths, timestep embedding 128, kernel 5, groups 8).

Three independently parameterized outcome members predict TCP pose, both object poses, and two finger positions (23 values). Each member encodes causal features through two 128-wide layers and applies two 256-wide layers to that encoding, a masked flattened action prefix and three deterministic slot-time features. Each predicts a residual in shared normalized state coordinates around the currently observed state. Residuals are multiplied by j/15. Slot zero is fixed to the current observation, because action slot zero is t-1 and its after-state is already observed at t. Outcome slot j uses only action slots 1..j. These are learned direct multi-horizon predictors, not a simulator or guaranteed recurrent physical dynamics. TCP quaternion labels are included; predicted quaternion geometry is unit-normalized, with sign-invariant orientation supervision.

## Losses, gates and actual gradients

All future-label losses use both action and future masks, excluding outcome slot zero. Pairwise action losses use both adjacent action masks. Future observations are training labels only and are never passed to diffusion forward or used as runtime inputs.

Total loss is epsilon MSE plus:

1. **0.25 dynamics loss.** Three action-conditioned predictions on demonstrated action chunks are supervised with Smooth L1 on the 11 normalized position/finger coordinates plus 0.25 times mean sign-invariant quaternion error `1-dot(q_pred,q_label)^2`. Independent 0.8-probability per-window bootstrap masks diversify members. Only this supervised term trains outcome-member parameters. The feature input is causal; it contains no future pose.
2. **0.05 phase BCE.** Predicted four-channel horizon logits are supervised on observed future contract predicates and the two physical phase labels. This trains the progress head and causal encoder. Diffusion loss also backpropagates through the predicted phase conditioning, so release/retreat state is not replaced by a hard phase controller.
3. **0.05 outcome consistency.** Form the clean DDPM action estimate, clip it to [-1,1] consistently with sampling, and predict its future states through the outcome members. Compare these predictions to real future labels using the same state error. Critically, the outcome weights and causal features are detached in this path, but the computations are NOT under no_grad: gradients pass through the action-conditioned predictor to clean actions, epsilon predictions and the denoiser. The model cannot reduce this term by simply changing its outcome weights to invent better futures.
4. **0.04 arriving-goal energy and 0.04 preservation energy.** For each member, denormalize each predicted object pose, normalize its wxyz quaternion, rotate all eight +/-0.020 m corners and find the largest absolute x and y corner offsets from the pad center. Signed slacks are 0.060 minus those maxima, and 0.011 minus center-z error. Base energy is the mean squared positive-part normalized slack violation. The optimized energy is its bounded transform E/(1+E), averaged over members. This is nonnegative and exactly zero over the full feasible region, not a center-distance loss. The all-corner max is piecewise differentiable; squared hinge is differentiable at zero. The arriving gate authorizes only slots whose real training future attains that object's goal while it is not already attained now, weighted by 0.5+0.5 times detached predicted goal probability. The preservation gate is the currently observed goal predicate for each color, applied to its predicted futures. Thus resting ungrasped blue away from its pad receives no instruction to slide toward the pad; red already placed remains context and a preservation target during blue handling. The observed gates are constants, but **the penalized poses are trainable action-conditioned predictions with a live gradient path to the denoiser**. This is not a loss computed only from observed poses.
5. **0.02 action-difference consistency.** Smooth L1 matches adjacent differences of the unclipped clean action estimate to demonstrated action differences. Weighting by alpha_bar squared suppresses high-noise amplification. It matches release edges instead of penalizing opening transitions indiscriminately; it is not an exact TCP-path objective.

Outcome consistency and energies are active only at alpha_bar >=0.5, weighted by alpha_bar squared. A detached action-support factor exp(-mean encoded action error / 0.25) suppresses very remote chunks. A detached per-object confidence factor exp(-(reference prediction error + ensemble disagreement)) uses positional scales [0.020,0.020,0.006] m. These are declared conservative support-proxy bandwidths, not fitted normalization scales or validated margins. Reference error uses true future labels only during training; disagreement is measured on candidate predictions. Detaching these weights prevents optimizing the gate instead of the prediction. Goal probabilities used in gates are detached so that lowering predicted progress cannot directly evade the energy. No confidence certificate is claimed. Normalizing energy losses by valid-slot count rather than active-gate count keeps rare attainment windows from dominating training. The broad denoising loss supervises every assigned phase, even when energy gates are off.

## Causal use, termination, and limitations

The model can take over at an open pickup, a lifted closed-finger state, a successful placement before opening, partial opening, or empty-hand movement toward blue insofar as these states resemble training support. qpos[7:9] in metres and qvel[16:18], changes in TCP/object world positions, TCP-to-object relation, goal slacks and persistent red pose distinguish these states. Approximately equal TCP/object motion with closed fingers supports a coupling inference; stationary pad objects while TCP rises and fingers open supports release. Neither establishes contact certainty. No phase is selected by demonstration index or elapsed time.

For a reusable physical handoff, continue opening/retreat if the active block remains coupled or fingers are only partly open. After red, a successor must see red left at its pad and the actual open-hand trajectory toward blue, or explicitly support taking over earlier to finish it. After blue, learned continuation opens and retreats toward the observed high TCP state while both objects remain placed. Actual observed rotated-corner containment independently determines task completion; neither the progress head nor rollout predictions can certify it. Optional physical-interface readiness must not be added to the task contract.

Only near-centered, almost upright successful demonstrations are supplied. The zero-energy pad region extends beyond demonstrated placements. No edge-placement robustness, invariance to changing layout, collision avoidance, recovery from failed grasps/releases, uncertainty calibration, or long-horizon stability is established. A predictor can hallucinate useful action effects away from the data despite freezing and support weighting. The short horizon and mostly static achieved objects may provide weak identification of perturbation dynamics. Dynamics consistency still favors expert outcomes even though geometric energy alone is tolerance-aware. Desired benefits are tolerance-aware placement shaping and preservation of completed goals, not a proven performance claim. Proposed falsification includes the matched no-energy auxiliary model and a center-distance baseline, followed by authorized edge/orientation variation testing.

Dependencies are only Torch, math and the public DiffusionBackbone/epsilon loss. Training is the fixed seed-0, 20,000-update, batch-128 AdamW recipe, horizon 16/history 2/execution 8, 100-step DDPM, EMA 0.999, and final-budget EMA selection. Interface/GPU checks establish executability, gradients, sampling and reload compatibility, not task success.

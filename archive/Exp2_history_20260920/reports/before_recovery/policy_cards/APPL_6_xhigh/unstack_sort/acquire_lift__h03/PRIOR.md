# acquire_lift__h03: executable clearance-guided action diffusion

## Assigned hypothesis and scope

The assigned heuristic is **Clear support before lateral loading**: once grasped near its source, a block should gain vertical separation before substantial sideways transport. The original statement is retained, not replaced by a graph prior, waypoint policy or temporal state machine. This implementation is Candidate A3: a learned response-space preference applied to learned diffusion actions. The scientific expectation is reduced lateral loading of the lower/support block at comparable pickup completion, not demonstrated safety or an established performance result.

The dataset and its M1_v2 expanded segmentation remain unchanged. All 24 assigned slices from the 12 original demonstrations train the same model, including initial open approach, local alignment, closure, separation, full lift, early sideways carry, and the long prior-red placement/opening/retreat/return-to-blue transition. There is no phase filtering, source-index feature, elapsed-time policy schedule, replay or scripted action controller. The fixed training budget is 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW at the supplied recipe, seed 0, last EMA selection. The repository retains DDPM 100-step noising/sampling, horizon 16, two observations, execution prefix 8 at 20 Hz, and decoding of seven absolute joint targets plus gripper.

## Causal representation and mandatory role adaptation

Deployment receives ONLY raw_history [B,2,47], noisy normalized actions and diffusion timesteps. There is no image, force, future state, segment identity, selected-object tag or invocation-tag argument. Consequently the heuristic's requested selected/previous role indicator is implemented explicitly as a **causal role proxy**, not claimed as an unavailable external tag. In this dataset red pickup remains far from red's goal, whereas prior-red-held entry already has red near its goal XY. A smooth sigmoid of red-to-red-goal XY distance centered at 0.12 m, temperature 0.018 m, yields selected-blue probability; its complement is selected-red. Previous-red proximity combines selected-blue with TCP-red proximity (0.055 m geometric kernel). A released-red proxy requires selected-blue, red low near its pad, observed finger width above about 0.060 m and TCP still in the local retreat region. Two geometric attachment proxies use measured width, TCP-object distance and stability of the TCP-object displacement across the two causal observations. These are uncertain attachment estimates, not physical contact sensors.

This resolves the observed two entry classes without forcing the resting blue to lift during unfinished red placement. It cannot resolve arbitrary different invocations at identical physical states. If the API asks for red again after red is already on its pad, or invokes an unseen reordering, this model lacks an explicit tag to disambiguate. That limitation should affect policy selection rather than silently treating the proxy as ground truth.

The flat encoder receives 124 features: all 94 globally normalized history components; 15 current relative-geometry components (TCP minus each object, red minus blue, each object minus its own goal); 9 normalized causal position differences; and the 6 explicit role/attachment/release features. Relative features use the corresponding shared full-data half-ranges, or the larger of the two object ranges for red-blue displacement. A two-layer 256-wide SiLU MLP produces a 256-dimensional global condition for the supplied FiLM Conditional U-Net (128/256/512 widths). Absolute world observations, goals, qpos and qvel remain present. No translation, yaw, arbitrary SO(3), joint-action equivariance or rotation invariance is claimed. World z is intentionally privileged.

The only observation/action normalizer is the supplied shared normalizer fit on all 8,729 samples of the 12 COMPLETE original trajectories. Nothing refits statistics on these slices. Quaternion channels retain unit-component bounds. Relative-feature scaling uses those same global scales. Distances such as 0.025 m below are physical energy tolerances, not fitted per-skill normalization scales.

## Trainable response ensemble

Two independent action-conditioned response modules of widths 128 and 160 receive the same causal feature vector and the candidate normalized absolute action sequence. Each has a two-layer history projection, action projection, learned within-chunk slot embedding and four causal residual temporal convolutions with dilations 1/2/4/8. These modules see the action prefix for each predicted slot, with receptive field covering the entire 16-action chunk. The slot embedding indexes the local prediction horizon, never a demonstration time or manipulation phase.

Each module predicts 18 residual coordinates: TCP displacement (3), red pose residual (position 3 plus quaternion components 4), blue pose residual (7), and total finger separation residual (1). Two additional logits estimate per-object attachment. Residuals are based on the current observed state. Position and quaternion residuals use the shared observation scales; width uses the sum of the two shared finger scales. Native predictions used by the energy are reconstructed in world metres. Quaternion labels are sign-aligned to the current causal quaternion. The predictor does not project predicted quaternions onto SO(3) or impose a hard rigid-grasp transform; their residual components receive supervision only. The energy uses positions and width, not an invented IK or analytic kinematic map.

Ground-truth future observations are training labels ONLY. Slots follow the exact interface alignment: action slot zero is t-1 and its outcome is the current state; subsequent labels are states after subsequent actions. All label terms use future_mask, and labels never cross a skill slice. Attachment soft labels combine future measured narrow fingers, local TCP-object proximity and relative displacement consistency between adjacent labels. They are pseudo-labels, not simulator contact truth. Learned attachment outputs are detached when used as energy gates, preventing an energy gradient from lowering the attachment gate to avoid its cost. The response heads are zero-initialized to an identity-response starting point, but every action-response map is trainable.

## Loss and gradient paths

1. **Diffusion**: the supplied masked epsilon MSE on all eight normalized action channels. This directly trains the condition encoder and U-Net over every expanded-slice phase.
2. **Response supervision**: ensemble-average masked Smooth L1 on the 18 normalized residual labels, plus 0.10 times masked binary cross-entropy on the two attachment logits. This term is weighted by 0.50. It trains both action-conditioned response modules with real demonstrated actions and futures, including previous-red descent and release.
3. **Frozen-response matching**: reconstruct the clean action estimate with the actual supplied alpha_bar, clip to the shared normalized action interval, pass it through both response modules with every response parameter detached, and match the mean normalized response to the future labels with Smooth L1. The weight is 0.05 times a response-training warmup ramp. Each example is additionally weighted by alpha_bar squared, with the denominator remaining the unweighted valid-label count. This limits high-noise amplification and encourages demonstrated upward progress, not merely staying still to achieve low lateral energy. Gradients flow through the action-conditioned response Jacobian into the denoiser; the response cannot alter itself to satisfy the denoiser's task loss.
4. **Soft response-space energy**: evaluated on those same denoised actions with frozen response parameters, future masks and slot zero excluded. Its weight is 0.02 times the same warmup, alpha_bar squared, and a detached ensemble-disagreement confidence. It is not a penalty computed only from observed poses. The path is epsilon prediction -> differentiable clean-action reconstruction -> frozen but action-differentiable response -> energy -> denoiser. The confidence and attachment gates cannot learn to turn themselves off through this loss.

The warmup ramp is zero through response update 500 and reaches one at update 2000. It does not filter training transitions; diffusion and response supervision train immediately on all examples. `prior_loss` is the sum of terms 2-4. Total loss is diffusion_loss + prior_loss. This remains a learned epsilon-prediction diffusion policy, not an auxiliary predictor with a scripted action output.

## Energy in physical coordinates

For red, predicted bottom clearance is predicted red center z minus 0.020 m minus (currently observed blue center z plus 0.020 m). For blue it is predicted blue center z minus 0.020 m above the table. The observed source support is deliberately held fixed inside each candidate evaluation so an imagined movement of the lower block cannot falsely create clearance. These are upright-block approximations, not rotated collision geometry.

The principal term is the sum, over selected attached roles, of causal near-source weight times `softplus((0.025 - predicted_clearance)/0.010)` times predicted horizontal object displacement squared. Deficit is smoothly capped as 8*tanh(deficit/8). Squared displacements are converted to a dimensionless cost at a 0.030 m physical tolerance and capped as 4*tanh(cost/4). Red near-source gating uses observed red-blue XY proximity, current low clearance and being away from red's goal. Blue gating uses low table clearance and distance from blue's goal; a source anchor for blue is not supplied, so the current blue state is the local displacement reference. Gating decays strongly above approximately 0.080 m source clearance. There is no requirement to reach the recorded 0.26 m lift before sideways motion. Full lifts and transport bands are learned from diffusion supervision.

A 0.25-weighted other-object stability term discourages predicted blue movement during red source acquisition, and predicted red movement during blue acquisition. It is gated with the same selected near-source roles, so it is not applied to red while prior-red placement is unfinished. A weak 0.10 transform-following term encourages equality of predicted selected-object and TCP displacement during inferred attachment. A weak 0.05 pinch term discourages opening the candidate gripper and large predicted width changes during selected attachment. These are soft terms, not a hard grasp or gripper command override; -1 during carry remains a learned command target.

For prior-red completion there is no selected-block lift term. Once red is observed low and the fingers have opened, release consistency penalizes predicted red movement, premature lateral TCP retreat while the TCP is still below about 0.065 m, and reclosing near the released red. Before opening, the red descent and opening command come from diffusion imitation and response matching. This avoids incorrectly pulling resting blue up, demanding that released red follow the retreat, or suppressing necessary open-finger alignment. Guidance is deliberately negligible when currently open and approaching a new object, even if a candidate predicts a future grasp: it waits for causal local grasp evidence.

## Deployment guidance and exact DDPM compatibility

The ordinary U-Net epsilon prediction is used unmodified during training, with the separate differentiable prior loss above. At deployment the same forward method adds inference-time guidance only for diffusion timesteps 0 through 24, where clean estimates are more credible. It does not replace the fixed DDPM transition.

Because the interface does not expose the scheduler's beta family, the model records per-timestep sums and counts of the supplied training alpha_bar into nontrainable checkpoint buffers. Their ratio is the actual fixed coefficient, not a fitted new schedule. The buffers contain no trajectory data. The ratio remains correct under either copying or averaging by EMA. If a timestep has not yet been observed, guidance at that timestep is skipped. Ordinary noising, posterior sampling, clipping and decoding always remain repository-owned.

The guide reconstructs a clean normalized candidate from epsilon using that coefficient, clips it, and differentiates the ensemble-mean predicted-response energy with respect to the candidate actions only. The gradient step strength is 0.02, scaled by min(response_updates/2000,1) and exp(-(ensemble positional RMS disagreement/0.015 m)^2). Its RMS action-coordinate displacement is capped at 0.005 and every coordinate at 0.015; slot zero is left alone. It checks the proposed response against the unguided response and accepts only a nonincreasing energy, finite gradient, both positional disagreements below 0.030 m, and fewer than 20 percent of original clean coordinates outside [-1.15,1.15]. Otherwise it returns the unguided epsilon for that candidate. The accepted clean-action change is converted exactly into an epsilon correction via sqrt(alpha_bar/(1-alpha_bar)). The sampler's clipping and global affine decoding are unchanged. The encoded range is the shared demonstrated native action range; no additional Panda joint-limit solver is claimed.

Local autograd is explicitly enabled even under an outer inference_mode; frozen response weights still transmit derivatives to candidate actions. Only low-noise steps incur this response-gradient computation. The two-module disagreement and one-step energy acceptance are conservative heuristics, not a proof that the learned response is calibrated or that an intervention is beneficial in the real environment. There is no hidden recurrent state, persistent saturation counter or automatic recovery controller.

## Handoff use and full-transition competence

Read HANDOFF.json alongside this document. The current physical observations allow takeover midway through either overlap: narrow fingers and stable local TCP-object offset indicate a possible held block; measured object height and causal co-motion disambiguate real separation from a commanded/predicted lift; near-goal red with closed fingers signals unfinished previous-object placement; wider fingers plus stationary red and upward separating TCP signal release and empty retreat. After that, the open TCP-blue relation and velocities locate the approach, and blue co-motion supports pickup readiness. All these phases receive ordinary diffusion training, even where the clearance prior is inactive.

Continue this policy if previous-red release, alignment, closure or actual separation is incomplete. Carry/place can inherit during the pickup overlap and complete the lift; it need not wait for a predetermined z or joint pose. Prefer transfer once the selected object visibly follows the TCP with narrow fingers and useful free-space lift, preserving the learned -1 carry command. Pass actual qpos/qvel, both object poses, TCP pose and finger positions plus the inference agent's selected-role intent, not a planned response pose. HANDOFF role intent is for the agent/successor interface; it is not an extra numeric input secretly given to this model.

Skill readiness never redefines task success. The supplied task contract is red_at_goal AND blue_at_goal using full rotated XY containment and z tolerance 0.011 m. It requires neither finger release, additional clearance nor a sustained hold. Intermediate release and pinch cues here concern useful transition behavior, not extra final success predicates.

## Limitations, dependencies and falsifiable comparisons

Only Torch/math and appl.public are needed. No images, contact/force channels, drawer dynamics, analytic IK/FK, obstacle map or test-time future labels are used. Vertical-first behavior may be expert-planner style rather than necessity. Learned dynamics from a narrow set of successful actions may ignore action effects or extrapolate incorrectly on denoised perturbations; two heads can agree and both be wrong. Open approaches are intentionally unguided, the role proxy has ordering-dependent ambiguity, and the model has no support for blocked vertical corridors, slips, unseen object rotations or an arbitrary new selected-role request. Soft energy can still slow approaches or encourage unnecessary lift. Width/near/co-motion gates cannot certify a grasp. No constraint or collision-safety guarantee is made.

Predeclared primary settings are the submitted pipeline values. Planned comparisons, NOT run here: (a) guidance_strength=0 with the same training energy to isolate inference guidance; (b) guidance_strength=0 and energy_weight=0 while retaining the response ensemble and response matching, to remove clearance guidance from training and inference; (c) other_stability_weight=0 to isolate the lower/other-block term; (d) smaller guidance strength 0.01 versus the primary 0.02. Compare pickup completion and observed support movement, and inspect frozen approaches, excess lift, wrong-role behavior, grasp slip, and command saturation. These are prospective ablations, not outcome claims. The interface check is only an executable-training/DDPM/EMA validation, not task-performance evidence.

# Prior implementation: carry_place_clear__h02

## Assigned research hypothesis (unchanged identity)

Heuristic 2 separates smooth seven-joint setpoint motion from discrete, state-dependent gripper events. It proposes phase-local hybrid diffusion with history-informed phase belief, persistent hold/open decisions, event/duration regularization and a measured PD-response auxiliary. It specifically warns that commanded absolute joint positions are not measured qpos, and that grasped finger widths are not the commanded closed target. This document describes our implementation choices; it does not modify the source heuristic.

The supplied controller is 20 Hz pd_joint_pos. Actions are seven **absolute** arm-joint targets in radians plus a gripper command. Per-finger commanded target is `0.025*g + 0.015` metres: -1 attempts -0.010 m, while +1 requests 0.040 m. Measured held fingers in inspected demonstrations are about 0.0182 m each. No kinematic conversion of setpoints into instantaneous TCP poses is assumed.

## Executable adaptation to the fixed interface

The public interface supplies only Gaussian DDPM noising/sampling, eight-dimensional epsilon prediction, and two raw causal state observations. It does not supply a categorical diffusion kernel, a custom final gripper decoder, previous issued actions, an invocation target tag, or persistent hidden-state transport. Consequently:

* Seven arm coordinates and the gripper coordinate have separate learned epsilon output heads but use the fixed joint 8D Gaussian DDPM. The gripper head shares a temporal trunk with a clean Bernoulli event classifier. Binary-support, clean-event consistency and persistence losses transfer categorical event information into the **actual gripper epsilon predictions**. This is not exact categorical diffusion and does not guarantee exactly +/-1 controller outputs.
* The GRU is reset for every call and processes precisely the two available observations. It is a finite-history encoder, not a long-running phase filter. Current and predicted-horizon phase probabilities are causal functions of this history. There is no external initialization to carry, no per-invocation timer and no source-index input.
* Red/blue identity is represented by learned global phase probabilities, inferred under the demonstrated red-first order from both objects, pads and TCP state. There is no explicit persistent target-tag channel. The inference agent can retain its own target bookkeeping, but cannot inject that tag into this model. Arbitrary blue-first invocation is not supported by the evidence.
* Semi-Markov motivation is implemented as weak forward-or-stay and stable-event curvature regularization, not an explicit duration distribution or hard monotone automaton. It cannot establish minimum safe dwell times. The mandated eight-action receding execution prefix is retained, not shortened.

All normalization buffers are copied from `spec['normalizer']`, which was fit on the 8,729 samples from the twelve COMPLETE original training demonstrations. No normalization is refit on this skill or on its overlaps. Shared `std` entries are half-ranges in this experiment, not statistical standard deviations. Quaternion components use the supplied unit bounds. No observation clipping or pose invariance is claimed. Raw metre thresholds used to construct weak training labels are semantic choices, not fitted feature scales or runtime constraints.

## Architecture and deployed information

Input: `[B,2,47]` raw observations, `[B,16,8]` corrupted normalized action chunk and diffusion timestep. Output: `[B,16,8]` epsilon. Deployment receives no future state, action target, mask, source index, label or success flag.

1. Normalize using shared buffers. Remove only the two zero drawer channels from the history input. A 45-to-128 embedding and 128-wide GRUCell process both observations. The final hidden state, current normalized observation and normalized two-frame difference feed a 192-wide context MLP. Qvel remains separately available, including the measured finger velocity channels. There is no explicit input-history mask in this API; any repeated boundary history is consumed as supplied.
2. A shared MLP combines context and learned action-slot embeddings to predict 15 phase logits for each of the 16 slots. Phases are red approach/close/lift/carry/descend-or-held-low/open/retreat; blue approach/close/lift/carry/descend-or-held-low/open/retreat; and blue terminal hold. Blue approach also covers the next-approach portion of the red invocation. This global ordering avoids treating the return toward blue as a backward jump within a single-block phase cycle.
3. Each soft phase vector maps through a learned eight-dimensional embedding. All 16 embeddings plus the 192 context coordinates form a 320-dimensional condition. The supplied conditional U-Net, widths 128/256/512, processes all eight noisy action coordinates and outputs the seven arm epsilon coordinates. Its unused eighth output is an artifact of the fixed-width building block, not another gripper controller.
4. A separate 128-wide gripper temporal branch receives the entire noisy chunk, the local 15-way phase probabilities and a relative slot coordinate. Four residual FiLM blocks have dilations 1,2,4,1. They are conditioned on the shared context/phase condition and learned sinusoidal diffusion-time embedding. It predicts gripper epsilon and a clean binary event logit. The two branches are coupled by the shared learned phase/context and the full noisy arm/gripper input; they are not independently sampled controllers. The event logit is not hard-thresholded at execution.
5. A training-only 64-to-128-to-128-to-9 response MLP receives normalized pre-action state, normalized command and nine physically interpreted setpoint-minus-measurement errors. For the seven arm errors it uses absolute targets; for the two finger errors it uses the actual per-finger target formula above. It predicts seven measured next-qpos displacements and two measured next finger positions. It is neither IK, FK nor a simulator, and is not used to execute a scripted correction.

The arm network and gripper network are both learned denoisers. There is no replay, action lookup, reference trajectory, external action controller, grasp detector claimed as ground truth, or post-sampling safety projection. A phase posterior can be obtained programmatically from `encode_history`, but the contracted deployment forward returns epsilon only. The successor should normally reinitialize from the same observations rather than assume automatic hidden-state transfer.

## Training labels, masks and losses

The fixed action horizon is aligned t-1 through t+14. Future-observation slot j is the observation **after** action j. The pre-state sequence is `[raw_history[0], future_obs[:-1]]`. For each response and phase target, valid action, future and predecessor masks are multiplied. Pair/triple regularizers multiply all participating masks. No target crosses a slice. The all-coordinate epsilon MSE uses the original action mask and is the primary objective.

Weak phase labels are generated only during training, from command sign and pre/post demonstrated poses. They use: pad proximity, object height, object/TCP finite-difference motion at 0.05 seconds, low-height held dwell, open near-pad motion, and the open return toward blue. In particular a closed command over a low near-pad object is descend/held-low, not open. A rising object supports lift; lateral motion at height supports carry. Red placed plus an open high TCP moving toward blue, or TCP sufficiently far from red, supports the blue-approach role. Blue placed plus high nearly stationary open TCP supports terminal hold. Labels have 0.04 uniform smoothing. World y near zero is a source-location proxy in the labeler; it is not a learned coordinate invariance. Label tolerances (for example 0.035/0.040 m pad proximity, 0.014 m vertical proximity, 0.085 m red horizontal clearance and 0.28 m terminal TCP height) are neither the exact rotated-containment contract nor deployment guards. Before/after following and release evidence provides weak supervision, not proof of contact. Ambiguous dwell boundaries and transition rates cannot be resolved perfectly from these successful traces.

Let `x0=(xt-sqrt(1-alpha_bar)*epsilon)/sqrt(alpha_bar)`. Scalar loss terms and weights are:

| Term | Weight | Gradient path and purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Masked 8D epsilon MSE | 1.0 | Both deployed epsilon heads, history encoder and phase conditioning |
| Weak phase cross-entropy | 0.08 | Causal horizon phase predictor and history encoder, not observed-pose constants |
| Adjacent phase order cost | 0.01 | Expected probability mass assigned to a backward step or jump greater than one; differentiates both adjacent predicted posteriors |
| Clean-gripper binary cross-entropy | 0.08 | Bernoulli logit, shared gripper temporal trunk and phase/history encoder |
| Clean gripper versus `2*sigmoid(logit)-1`, smooth L1 | 0.025 | Both actual gripper epsilon and event classifier; weighted by alpha_bar |
| Binary-support squared distance `(abs(x0_g)-1)^2` | 0.01 | Actual gripper epsilon; auxiliary x0 clipped to [-2,2] and weighted by alpha_bar |
| Gripper first-difference matching, smooth L1 | 0.02 | Actual adjacent gripper epsilon predictions; matches demonstrated changes rather than suppressing legitimate switches |
| Arm first-difference matching, smooth L1 | 0.01 | Actual adjacent arm predictions; encourages expert-like setpoint continuity, not zero motion |
| Binary probability second-difference penalty | 0.01 | Predicted event probabilities, only where three consecutive demonstrated signs agree; discourages rapid unsupported reversals |
| Teacher-command measured-response fit | 0.10 | Response MLP, using demonstrated commands and measured next states |
| Denoised-command measured-response coupling | 0.05 | Through response MLP into denoised arm/gripper actions and thus deployed denoisers; same measured labels |

For response prediction, seven arm displacement targets are divided by shared qpos half-ranges and next finger positions use shared mean/half-range. Mean smooth-L1 arm displacement error is multiplied by 16 to balance this small-displacement task against finger-position prediction. This is a task loss weight, not a local normalizer. No model parameters are detached in response coupling; fitting teacher commands anchors the response predictor, although co-adaptation remains a limitation. Future states used as response pre-states are teacher-forced training information only; the model never rolls out future observations at deployment.

The clean consistency, support, first-difference and denoised-response losses are multiplied by alpha_bar without renormalizing away this downweighting. Alpha is lower-bounded only for numerical division. First-difference and response auxiliaries use x0 locally clipped to [-2,2]; clean gripper consistency uses robust smooth L1 on unclipped x0. This limits high-noise amplification. Such auxiliary clipping does not replace the fixed DDPM sampler or its clip_sample setting. Every nonzero prior term depends on trainable outputs. Computing weak labels under no-grad is intentional and does not make the supervised prediction losses constant.

Two observations cannot prove attachment or a failed release; all uncertainty and off-distribution recovery remain the responsibility of external observation and policy selection.

## Invocation and termination guidance

Use this policy for in-progress acquisition through placement and clearance, in the demonstrated unstack red-then-blue order. Continue it through unfinished finger closure, held lift, transport, descent and release lag. For red, a useful physical transfer has the object staying on its pad while fingers open and TCP separates upward/returns toward blue. An earlier transfer is possible within the shared release/retreat overlap if the compatible successor also infers that phase and continues opening/clearance. Do not switch the target merely because red is geometrically at the goal while still grasped. The successor needs the same two raw observations, object identities/goals and external invocation bookkeeping, not a fixed open-forever mode. It must later decide blue closure from alignment and motion.

For blue, retain the demonstrated open retreat toward about 0.300 m and terminal hold if clearance continuation is requested. There is no preset skill schedule or internal termination command. The inference API chooses invocation duration. Replanning is the unchanged 8-action prefix of a 16-action DDPM chunk.

Task achievement is separate: both blocks must satisfy full rotated XY containment on their own pads, half extents 0.060 m with object half-size 0.020 m, and center-z tolerance 0.011 m around target z 0.020 m. Red target is (-0.24,-0.25,0.02); blue is (-0.24,0.25,0.02), world metres. The supplied contract requires one observation, not release, TCP clearance, velocity limits or a sustained hold. Our physical handoff recommendations do not change that contract.

## Dependencies, applicability and limitations

Dependencies are Torch and the supplied `appl.public` numerical U-Net/epsilon-loss helpers. No images or image encoder enter this state experiment. Train with the fixed 20,000 updates, batch 128, seed 0, AdamW 1e-4/1e-6, cosine with 500 warmup updates, gradient clipping 1, EMA 0.999 and 100-step DDPM. Select last EMA at that predeclared budget. Checks test execution, gradients and reload only; they are not performance evaluations.

Binary control, successful-state phase proxies, nearly fixed event timing and a two-frame history are strong restrictions. Weak monotone regularization may penalize real faster transitions or recovery reversals. Binary support is a soft learned preference and continuous decoding may still produce intermediate commands or chatter. The response model may co-adapt or be inaccurate away from teacher commands; it cannot certify contact, friction or dynamics. No interrupted-placement, failed-release or alternative-controller evidence establishes recovery. Different controller dynamics require rechecking absolute-target tracking and finger response. Target-role inference may fail outside the red-first order or if an object is dropped. No calibrated confidence, constraint satisfaction, equivariance or optimal binary action result is claimed.

Scientific prediction, not a measured result: phase and event organization should reduce gripper reversals and improve transfer continuity without delaying pad attainment. Appropriate future comparisons are ordinary continuous 8D diffusion, phase-agnostic binary-auxiliary diffusion, and ablations of phase ordering and response coupling. No such experiments are claimed here.

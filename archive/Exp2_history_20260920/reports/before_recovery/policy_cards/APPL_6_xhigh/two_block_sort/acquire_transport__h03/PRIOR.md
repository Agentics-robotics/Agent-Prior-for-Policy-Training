# acquire_transport__h03: executable height-separated waypoint hierarchy

## Assigned hypothesis and scope

The assigned heuristic is **Height-separated waypoint hierarchy** (heuristic 3): contact approach/lift and free-space retargeting occupy different geometric regimes, even when their durations differ. The research hypothesis is that generating a compact role-relative geometric plan before actuator targets can reduce low-height lateral shortcuts and improve learning of the long release-to-next-pick bridge. This document records implementation choices and limitations; it does not replace or modify the source heuristic.

This policy trains on **every supplied sample in all 24 expanded acquire_transport slices**, without truncation to a narrower pickup phase, hand-authored action targets, or replay. The second occurrence begins while red may still be held and descending over its goal. It includes predecessor lowering and release, vertical separation, high retargeting, blue approach/closure and the full blue lift into initial goalward transport. The first occurrence covers initial high empty approach through red pickup and early transport. Overlap is training coverage, not a prescribed switch schedule.

## Necessary adaptations to the available interface

1. **Local rather than complete event plans.** The only future labels available to compute_loss are 16 states after the action slots t-1 through t+14. There is no full remaining segment, event annotation or duration oracle in a minibatch. The implementation therefore diffuses four ordered **local arc-time knots** and positive durations, replanning from current state on every forward. It does not claim to predict all distant release-clearance/source-hover/source-low/lift events. The same geometric hierarchy is trained throughout those phases, with a learned phase selector and soft height organization. Events outside the window are not fabricated.
2. **Roles inferred, not supplied.** Forward receives two 47-dimensional observations, not caller role tokens. In the demonstrated red-first order, red remains far from its pad through the first acquire exit, whereas second-occurrence entry already has red aligned with its pad in XY even when its Z is high. The fixed *feature-association* predicate `red_goal_XY_distance < 0.075 m` selects blue as requested, otherwise red. The corresponding predecessor is red only in the blue occurrence. This predicate never returns actions or selects a script. It is not a completion test and does not require release. Arbitrary blue-first or caller-overridden roles are unsupported by this interface adaptation.
3. **A small auxiliary diffusion, unchanged action DDPM.** High-level geometry has its own eight-step cosine Gaussian diffusion, trained with clean-sample regression at random noise levels. For economical, repeatable conditioning at all 100 action-denoising calls, its deployment decoder follows eight deterministic posterior-mean updates from zero (the mean of the Gaussian starting distribution), without posterior innovations. This is a mean-decoded diffusion plan, not an exact stochastic high-level posterior sample. Direct rollout supervision reduces train/decode mismatch. The original eight-dimensional actions remain a genuinely learned epsilon-prediction DDPM with the supplied 100 training/inference steps and all fixed optimizer/EMA settings.
4. **Reference conditioning, not IK.** No robot model is provided. The tracker learns seven original absolute Panda joint targets and the gripper command from demonstration actions. Generated geometry is neither an IK command nor a verified prediction of executed kinematics. Quaternion orientation is retained in state and generated knots; no task or joint-action equivariance is claimed.

## Causal representation and learned architecture

Forward uses only raw_history [B,2,47], noisy_action [B,16,8] and diffusion timestep. State includes qpos (nine positions including two fingers), qvel (nine velocities), world TCP pose, both object poses (metres, wxyz), and both labelled world goals. Drawer compatibility channels remain present and constant. There are no images or persistent progress counters.

The supplied shared complete-demonstration normalizer is registered as immutable buffers. No scales are fitted inside the model. Full observations retain their shared affine coordinates, including absolute joints and quaternion unit bounds. Relative vectors use the shared TCP XYZ half ranges, not narrow red/blue Y ranges or per-skill standard deviations. These vectors are TCP-to-requested-object, requested-object-to-goal, TCP-to-red-pad, and TCP-to-red-object. Both causal histories and the role indicator enter a 120-to-256 MLP. Absolute world Z is never discarded.

A learned eight-way phase head predicts predecessor lowering, predecessor release, empty vertical separation, high retarget/hover, requested approach, closure, lift and early loaded transport. Its probabilities condition both networks. The sum of the first three probabilities, gated by predecessor existence, softly mixes predecessor-pad XY with current requested-object XY to choose the geometric coordinate anchor. This is an observation-conditioned estimate of progress, not an initialized first waypoint. Weak labels train it; inference uses only its predictions. Current heights, finger opening, TCP/object relations, qvel and two-frame motion information disambiguate partly completed transitions.

The high-level MLP takes the noised four-by-15 plan, a sinusoidal noise embedding, current encoded poses and the 264-dimensional state/phase context. Each knot contains TCP position/quaternion, requested-object position/quaternion and log duration. Positions have role-relative XY and **world Z expressed using the shared TCP Z mean and half range**. Both objects use these same positional units in the plan. Quaternion signs in targets are aligned to current observed orientation. Each clean denoising prediction adds bounded learned position and quaternion residuals to current poses and renormalizes quaternions. Position bounds are twice the shared Cartesian half ranges relative to current state; these are representation bounds, not collision constraints. Log durations are bounded to [-2,2], giving strictly positive `4*exp(log_duration)` sample intervals. Seconds are not assumed.

At deployment an eight-step geometric posterior-mean recursion produces the knots. A piecewise cubic smoothstep interpolator starts at the **live current TCP/requested-object poses** at slot zero. Increasing cumulative durations order the knots. The interpolator is a convex interpolation between adjacent position knots, hence does not overshoot those knots; predicted knots themselves can still be unsafe. Quaternions are renormalized after interpolation. It is C1 in position, with zero tangent at knot boundaries, not a velocity-constrained path. The low-level network also sees observed qvel and is not forced to reproduce those zero tangents. Replanning avoids replaying an old start knot, but does not mathematically guarantee continuous executed motion.

Sixteen interpolated pose samples and four log durations enter a 128-dimensional learned reference encoder. State (256), phase (8) and reference (128) form a 392-dimensional condition for the supplied action U-Net (128/256/512 widths). The network always predicts action epsilon, never direct scripted actions. No IK, FK, images or external solvers are dependencies. Only torch, math and appl.public are imported.

## Training labels and losses

Action slot j is t-1+j, and future_obs[j] is the state after that action. Thus future slot zero is the current-time state, consistent with anchoring the interpolator at time zero. Future masks never cross the expanded slice boundary. Knot targets use the available within-window TCP and requested-object trajectory. Each valid consecutive pair contributes normalized TCP displacement magnitude plus half the requested-object displacement magnitude plus 0.03 time mass. Cumulative quartiles select four ordered indices. The time mass preserves stationary closure/release labels. Indices are made strictly increasing where support permits; repeated/unavailable terminal knots are masked. Durations are successive selected index differences, in sample intervals, encoded as log(duration/4). Incomplete windows train only their supported targets. Quaternion signs are aligned to the causal pose before regression.

All reductions mask invalid actions or futures, including pair masks for increment losses. Nonexistent phase events are not inserted. All prior terms below act on **trainable predictions**:

- **Action diffusion:** primary masked epsilon MSE uses exactly the causal inference pathway, including the generated reference. A second tracker view receives masked labelled reference poses plus 0.02 normalized Gaussian perturbations and duration logs plus 0.04 noise; unsupported slots use detached predictions. Its target is the same original action noise. `L_diffusion = (L_causal + 0.15 L_teacher_reference)/1.15`. This exposes the tracker to labelled/noisy references as well as imperfect predicted plans. It does not invent outcome labels for perturbed references.
- **Geometric diffusion, weight 0.15:** masked clean-plan MSE after randomly noising target plans at one of eight auxiliary noise levels. This is x0-parameterized conditional denoising regression (equivalent to noise regression with the corresponding noise-level weighting), not unweighted epsilon MSE. It trains the geometric diffusion MLP and state/phase conditioning.
- **Geometric rollout, weight 0.15:** the actual deterministic decoded knots regress the same masked targets, backpropagating through all eight decoding steps.
- **Reference trajectory, weight 0.30:** interpolated TCP/requested-object poses regress all masked future poses, training geometry, durations, interpolation timing and the progress/context network.
- **Phase cross entropy, weight 0.05:** weak current-observation phase labels derived from role, finger mean opening, TCP/object proximity and heights supervise the learned phase head. These labels are not measured contact truth. Predecessor labels take precedence over requested approach/lift, preventing a still-held red state from being labelled blue pickup. The causal phase classifier is also trained indirectly by action/reference losses.
- **Soft geometry, weight 0.10:** (a) approach/closure TCP lateral error to the labelled requested object is penalized only beyond 15 mm slack; (b) empty separation/high-travel predicted XY step energy is multiplied by squared predicted clearance deficit below 0.25 m; (c) requested lift/early-transport predicted XY step energy is multiplied by squared predicted requested-object height deficit below 0.25 m; (d) a soft predicted TCP floor discourages heights below 0.005 m. Cartesian errors use shared positional scales. Training-only future phase labels gate these terms. Predecessor lowering/release is exempt from the empty-travel and requested-lift terms. The floor still applies. Gradients reach predicted poses, durations, phase/context and geometric denoising layers; observed gates alone are not being minimized.
- **Actuator increment matching, weight 0.02:** reconstruct clean encoded actions from the primary predicted epsilon using the supplied alpha_bar, compare consecutive increments to demonstrated encoded-action increments using smooth L1, and weight by alpha_bar to suppress high-noise amplification. This is not a generic zero-velocity penalty and does not impose FK consistency. Gradients reach the action diffusion and its conditioning hierarchy.

`loss = diffusion_loss + prior_loss`, where prior_loss is the weighted sum above. The primary action objective is always causal and gradients flow from epsilon prediction through the reference encoder and geometric rollout. Geometry has direct future supervision rather than an observed-pose-only constant penalty. Training is seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, history 2, horizon 16, execution 8, AdamW 1e-4/1e-6, cosine schedule/500 warmup, norm clipping 1 and last EMA .999. Validation does not establish task performance or justify selecting a checkpoint by performance.

## Full-slice handoff interpretation and observed support

Observed examples, all within assigned action ranges:

- demo10000/0 has TCP Z 0.442 m and fingers 0.04 m each. /80 and /100 descend near red with fingers open; TCP Z is about 0.066 and 0.020 m. /125 has fingers approximately 0.0183 m, red Z 0.023 m. /150 red and TCP rise together to about 0.191 m, and /170 reach about 0.279 m. /184 retains the grasp-like relation and has begun goalward translation.
- demo10000/265 and demo10001/260 have red aligned to its goal XY while TCP/red are still at approximately 0.209 m, fingers loaded and action -1. The requested object is **blue**, but the immediate motion still lowers red. demo10011/272 shows the same entry at TCP Z 0.127 m. Measured expanded-slice starts support predecessor heights roughly 0.126-0.253 m, not a fixed entry waypoint.
- demo10000/300 has red at its pad at Z 0.020 m, TCP about 0.023 m and opening fingers about 0.0371 m. /315 shows almost fully open fingers and emerging vertical retreat. /350 has TCP Z 0.298 m with red remaining on the pad. /400 is high empty lateral retargeting; /424 is near blue hover at about 0.267 m. These bridge phases must not be skipped merely because red satisfies a goal predicate.
- demo10000/490 approaches blue low with fingers open; /525 is closed with incipient object lift; /550 shows blue/TCP co-motion near 0.217 m. /570 and /584 reach approximately 0.28 m, with /584 already translating. demo10001/579 and demo10011/583 show similar high loaded, goalward-moving exits. TCP X is often 8-10 mm offset from object center, motivating nonzero approach slack rather than exact centering.

Handoff ranges overlap delivery through predecessor release/retreat/high retargeting and through requested approach/closure/lift. The phase/context input permits takeover in the middle of these transitions without a persistent first-knot assumption. Exit readiness uses **observed** object elevation and co-motion, not just a predicted waypoint or narrow fingers. Narrow fingers and approximately constant TCP/object offsets suggest a grasp but are not direct force/contact measurements. qvel and the second causal pose provide motion direction, while red/blue pose and goal fields prevent predecessor/request confusion in the demonstrated role order.

Usually continue this policy while the predecessor still needs safe lowering/release/retreat, or while the requested object is being approached/closed/lifted. Transfer to delivery once observed carrying near the demonstrated 0.28 m region is established and motion toward the requested goal has begun; delivery may also take over earlier in the shared pickup/lift coverage and finish the lift. Pass live qpos/qvel, TCP and both object poses/goals, inferred roles and the grasp-maintaining command/context, not this model's old plan. Replan promptly and avoid pulling back to stale knots. No source-index schedule is used at deployment.

## Applicability, success and limitations

Use in the observed unobstructed tabletop setting, downward-facing TCP and nearly upright cubes with enough clearance for approximately 0.28-0.30 m high movement. Low ceilings, sliding tasks, nonvertical entanglement, obstructed paths, large off-demonstration states, arbitrary role order and corrective regrasp are outside the evidence. The vertical organization may be expert-specific. Thresholds for labels, role association and soft losses are implementation design choices, not guarantees or hard rejection rules. The model can ignore or mistrack a reference; actuator smoothness matching and geometric supervision do not establish physical safety or grasp stability.

A posterior emphasizing blue pickup while still needing red lowering, predicted lift without observed object lift, low lateral motion before release/lift, table-directed knots, large joint-target jumps despite smooth geometry, or regressions to old path starts are failure signatures. There is no implemented recovery controller. The long transition is represented by successive local plans rather than a global event guarantee; stochastic high-level plan uncertainty is collapsed during decoding. No ablation or performance result is claimed. Useful future comparisons would be a flat action DP, the hierarchy without geometric penalties, and this full candidate at the identical budget, measuring path height, tracking/smoothness and observed handoff completion.

Skill readiness does **not** redefine task success. The supplied contract is red_at_goal AND blue_at_goal, with full rotated XY containment on separate pads centered at red (-0.18,-0.25,0.02) and blue (-0.18,0.25,0.02), pad half XY 0.06 m, block half-size 0.02 m and Z tolerance 0.011 m. One observation suffices. No additional release, TCP clearance, velocity threshold or sustained hold is required by that task contract.

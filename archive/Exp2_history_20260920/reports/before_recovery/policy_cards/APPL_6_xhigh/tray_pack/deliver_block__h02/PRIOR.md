# Rim-aware transport topology: executable prior for deliver_block__h02

## Assigned hypothesis and scope

The original assigned heuristic is **Rim-aware transport topology** (heuristic 2): lateral payload transfer should occur above tray walls, with low motion reserved for aligned acquisition or insertion. Its proposed mechanism is fixture-conditioned predictive ranking of joint-action diffusion samples. Its original statement, evidence, applicability and limitations remain the assignment; this document describes our implementation choices, not a replacement heuristic.

The policy learns **all 24 expanded deliver_block segments**, without trimming overlaps, conditioning on source index, phase-based sample filtering, a preset skill schedule, or replay. The fixed learned action distribution is over 16 slots of eight shared-normalized native controls: seven absolute Panda joint targets and the original gripper command (larger means more open). The repository performs DDPM sampling, bounded decoding and execution of eight steps. Nothing in this package emits Cartesian controls or implements IK.

## Necessary interface adaptation

The only deployed hook is `forward(noisy_action, timestep, raw_history) -> epsilon`. It cannot intercept completed DDPM samples, run a multi-chunk search, change the sampler, or choose the execution prefix. Consequently **online multi-sample rollout ranking, likelihood-based candidate rejection, and inference-time energy guidance are not implemented or claimed**. Instead we amortize a *one-sided predictive energy preference* into the learned diffusion model during training: a denoised action estimate should not have larger predicted geometry energy than the demonstrated action chunk by more than a small tolerance. An action-conditioned future-pose ensemble supplies the differentiable connection from joint actions to cube geometry. Fixture-relative inputs and supervised causal mode forecasts condition the deployed denoiser. Thus the implemented distinction from raw-joint DP is a fixture-aware predictive training bias, not a different action coordinate system or a scripted controller.

This is a falsifiable adaptation, not evidence that training-time preference is equivalent to deployment search. A matched geometry-free pose-supervision ablation, raw-joint DP, and controlled fixture changes would be needed to distinguish the geometric term's contribution. Those experiments are not performed here. The fixed 20,000-update budget trains all terms together; no separate successful fine-tuning experiment is claimed.

## Causal information, frames and normalization

Inputs are two causal 47-dimensional measured states. World positions are in metres; quaternions are wxyz. The model uses qpos 0:9, qvel 9:18, TCP 18:25, red pose 25:32, blue pose 32:39, and red/blue goals 41:44 and 44:47. Drawer compatibility channels are zero and are not interpreted as a moving fixture. There are no images, external contact labels, absolute time, source trajectory IDs, or future observation inputs at deployment.

All raw observations use the supplied complete-demonstration mean/half-range buffers, with fixed unit bounds for quaternion components. Actions remain in the repository's shared affine range representation. No normalizer is fitted, reweighted or narrowed on this skill. Derived position differences use the shared TCP xyz half ranges; pose prediction residuals and supervised position errors use the supplied per-pose xyz half ranges. Explicit metre tolerances in geometric energies are engineering penalty lengths, not fitted data normalization.

The five supplied fixture boxes are stored as configuration-derived buffers, with floor first. Their side-wall tops are 0.09 m. The state interface has no runtime fixture fields: using another tray would require deliberately supplying correct geometry in the configuration/model buffers and evaluating or retraining. We do not infer unseen fixtures. The fixed red/blue channel order provides color identity tokens; an external selected-payload or pending-role token is not available. Instead the learned mode forecasts and object-goal/TCP relations provide soft role information for both cubes. This does not impose a hard red-before-blue control rule, though the training routes are nominally in that order.

Two states expose TCP direction, object co-motion and finger opening change in addition to explicit qvel. They distinguish open downward acquisition from closed co-moving lift, closed goal-aligned lowering, released objects remaining still while TCP rises, and subsequent open traversal. Attachment is only inferred: in demo10300, held red has TCP about 9.5 mm to one side with essentially equal center height; a model must not assume the TCP sits a fixed large distance above the cube. Both measured object poses remain available to preserve the packed cube.

## Architecture

1. A 231-dimensional feature vector contains both normalized states (94), their difference (47), both-history TCP/object and goal relations plus inter-object displacement (30), and relative fixture-center/half-extent features for TCP and both cubes (60). All five boxes, including the floor, participate.
2. A 256-wide two-layer encoder with LayerNorm produces the main causal context. A 192-hidden mode forecaster outputs nine probabilities for each of 16 slots: for each object, inferred attachment, free lateral carry, low aligned insertion, and settled/released; plus empty-hand low retreat. A learned projection of this forecast provides another 64 context channels. The 320-dimensional condition enters the supplied 128/256/512 conditional U-Net. Epsilon prediction is the only deployed action-generation mechanism.
3. Two independently initialized MLP rollout members take the same nonlearned causal feature vector and the proposed 16x8 normalized action chunk. Each has two 384-wide hidden layers and predicts residual world TCP/red/blue positions and quaternion components at all 16 slots. Position residuals are scaled by the shared pose half ranges. Quaternion residuals are added to measured current quaternions and unit-normalized. This is a learned finite-horizon action/chunk predictor, not kinematics or a simulator. It is allowed to inspect the entire proposed action chunk, but never future observations as inputs. There is no claim of causal dynamics factorization within a proposed chunk.

Slot 0 corresponds to action t-1 and its target is state t, already present as the latest causal observation; later slots predict states t+1 through t+15. Predictors are anchored at state t, so slot 0 should learn a zero residual. All label losses respect action/future masks and do not cross assigned slice boundaries. Padding is not interpreted as a successful settled state.

## Supervision and gradient paths

The total objective is

`L = L_epsilon + 0.25 L_rollout + 0.05 L_mode + 0.05 L_clean_pose + 0.03 L_geometry_preference`.

- **Epsilon:** masked MSE against fixed DDPM noise, using the supplied normalization, 100 diffusion steps and standard U-Net recipe. Gradients update the encoder, mode forecast/projection and denoiser.
- **Rollout:** masked shared-scale position MSE plus 0.2 times sign-invariant quaternion chordal error `1 - dot(q_pred,q_label)^2`, with 0.5 times a consecutive-position-increment MSE. Both masks must be valid for an increment target. Loss is averaged across ensemble members. Gradients update the rollout weights from real demonstration actions and future states.
- **Mode forecast:** masked binary cross entropy to soft geometric proxies computed from training future states and increments. Inferred attachment uses total finger width near/below 0.055 m and TCP/object distance near/below 0.035 m. Free carry additionally uses lateral object displacement around 0.0004 m per recorded slot and nonalignment to the goal. Insertion requires inferred attachment, xy goal alignment around 0.035 m and center z near/below 0.080 m. Settled labels require xy/z agreement and either opening or TCP separation. Empty retreat labels use opening, a settled object and low TCP. These smooth lengths are weak supervision choices, not calibrated contact detectors or deployment applicability thresholds. Modes are forecasts from causal history, not a hard time schedule.
- **Clean-pose consistency:** reconstruct `x0` from epsilon and batch alpha_bar, clamp to the sampler's normalized [-1,1] bounds, and pass it through both rollout members. Their linear weights and input features are **detached for this branch**. Future-pose error therefore trains the denoiser through predicted actions without letting the rollout weights change to flatter a candidate. The independently supervised rollout branch above remains trainable.
- **Geometry preference:** evaluate the candidate and real-action predicted rollouts under the same detached causal mode forecasts. Penalize `relu(E_candidate - stopgrad(E_reference) - 0.01)`. The reference is the ensemble's prediction for the demonstrated chunk, not an unlabelled counterfactual action. It absorbs nominal conservative geometry costs rather than forcing every short chunk to finish the task. Gradients flow through predicted cube/TCP positions and orientations, frozen rollout operations, reconstructed actions and epsilon back into the denoiser. Modes, reference energy, and confidence are detached in this branch; they cannot reduce this loss simply by shutting off the gate. This is a comparative soft energy regularizer, not a collision constraint.

Both clean-pose and energy terms are weighted by alpha_bar and turned off at alpha_bar <= 0.2 to limit high-noise amplification. The detached training confidence is `exp(-fit_MSE/0.020^2 - candidate_disagreement_MSE/0.015^2)`, where fit is the ensemble's masked position error on the actual future labels, and disagreement is masked candidate position disagreement between the two members. No future-label confidence gate is used in deployed forward. Ensemble agreement is a heuristic uncertainty measure and can be confidently wrong.

## Implemented geometry energy

For each predicted object quaternion, all eight +/-0.02 m cube corners are rotated and translated. Their world-axis envelope supplies extents and bottom height. We implement:

- squared conservative envelope overlap with all five fixture boxes, with **zero floor buffer** to permit supported placement and a 3 mm soft side-wall buffer;
- conservative cube/cube envelope overlap, retaining both cubes even when one is packed;
- mode-weighted free-lateral-carry bottom-height cost below wall top plus the buffer, suppressed by insertion mode;
- a misplaced-descent term coupling predicted downward increments to corner-based xy containment violation during free carry;
- last-valid-step corner-based xy containment and center-z tolerance error, gated by forecast low-insertion/settled probabilities rather than treating every chunk as a terminal placement horizon;
- movement of a cube already near its goal in the causal state;
- a weak empty-hand TCP proxy penalizing lateral motion below wall-top plus 0.025 m during learned low-retreat mode.

Containment energy uses the contract's 0.06 m region half extents and 0.011 m center-z tolerance. Envelopes are conservative for rotated cubes: this is not a full separating-axis OBB collision test. There is no continuous swept-volume test, gripper geometry or robot-link model. The 3 mm margin is an unvalidated regularization choice, not a certified clearance requirement. The floor penalty permits intentional support; acquisition/lift and insertion are not globally prohibited by a universal carry-height constraint. A constant cost on observed poses alone would not train diffusion; all action preferences above depend on trainable predicted actions passed through a learned differentiable predictor.

At 120 the command is close, total measured width about 0.0365 m and TCP/object center heights about 0.0207/0.0208 m. At 160 red rises to 0.274 m and at 240 it is about 0.309 m above the goal. The assigned red segment ends at 415, not at initial containment.

Blue entry 445 similarly begins an open descent at TCP z 0.1158 m. Indices 480/520/561 cover close, lift and lateral carry while red stays packed. Blue lowers through 640/655, releases by 665, retreats at 680 and holds with open fingers near TCP z 0.3165 m at 720/741. Demo10307 repeats this topology with different source positions: red 72->110->165->259->287->335->407 and blue 439->477->530->632->641->659->700->729. These index sequences are evidence, never inputs or a controller schedule.

Measured overlap ranges were read separately. For demo10300 they include 75:182 with acquisition, 265:415 for the outgoing shared transition, and 445:562 for blue acquisition. The equivalent demo10307 ranges are 72:178, 259:408 and 439:552. Measured expanded-start widths are approximately 0.080 m and start TCP z ranges about 0.0877-0.1779 m; measured expanded-end widths are again about 0.080 m and TCP z ranges about 0.2671-0.3165 m. These are empirical support, not hard thresholds or proofs of grasp. No filtering removes the acquisition completion, release, settling, retreat or outgoing open approach from diffusion supervision.

Continue this policy when entered partway through a supported descent/close/lift or through lowering/release/retreat. A successor taking over early must receive actual state/history and inferred color/manipulation status and be able to continue that motion. In particular, a cube can satisfy geometric containment while fingers still hold it near z 0.0457 m. Later open/settled and TCP rising/clear cues make transfer operationally more useful. `HANDOFF.json` states the measured cues and limitations in selector-facing form.

## Success, applicability and limitations

Formal success remains exactly the supplied contract: full rotated xy containment and center-z tolerance for red AND blue in their own regions, required for one observation. Release, low velocity, stability duration, sustained hold, and TCP clearance are **not** added predicates. Open-hand retreats are demonstrated continuation/readiness states, not a redefinition of success.

This policy is applicable to the supplied open tray and nominal supported acquisition/carry/lower/release transitions. Changing fixture heights, goals, role order, or starting after a slip is not validated. Two causal observations do not establish true contact or attachment. A rollout MLP can use state shortcuts rather than learning causal action effects; nominal demonstration supervision and two-member agreement cannot certify predictions for novel chunks. Frozen predictor weights reduce a direct cheating gradient but do not prevent action-model exploitation. The observed high waypoint may be generic rather than caused by the rim; no collision labels or alternative-height demonstrations establish its necessity. Learned absolute-joint outputs are neither translation nor rotation equivariant. TCP/cube geometry does not guarantee arm or gripper clearance. There is no scripted recovery, replay, search, IK or image encoder hidden in this implementation.

Training uses the assigned seed 0, batch 128, horizon 16, history 2, execution 8, 20,000 updates, AdamW 1e-4 with weight decay 1e-6, 500 warmup steps and cosine schedule, gradient clipping 1, and EMA 0.999. Selection is the last EMA at this predeclared budget. Interface checks are smoke tests only; no task-performance feedback was available during design.

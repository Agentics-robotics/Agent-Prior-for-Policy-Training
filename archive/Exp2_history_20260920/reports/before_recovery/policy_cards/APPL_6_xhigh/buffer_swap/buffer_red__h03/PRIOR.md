# H03: route geometry separated from contact-scale joint refinement

## Identity and research hypothesis

Policy: `buffer_red__h03`, skill: `buffer_red`, heuristic index: 3, experiment: M1_v2. The supplied source heuristic is not modified. Its statement is: "Separate free-space route geometry from contact-scale motor detail: model lift/transit/descent/retreat as a coarse task-space path and learn a conditional joint-space refinement. Hypothesis: the hierarchy reduces long-horizon coordination errors without replaying exact durations."

This package implements that hypothesis as a **learned hierarchical action diffusion policy**, not a scripted geometric controller. The coarse path is a trained diffusion representation that actually conditions motor denoising during both training and deployment. The final outputs remain eight-channel epsilon estimates for seven absolute Panda joint targets plus the gripper command. The outer, fixed DDPM produces the actions. A learned action-to-TCP trajectory predictor supplies differentiable geometry/motor supervision; it is not an IK solver or analytic forward-kinematics service.

## Executable adaptations to the research proposal

1. The interface fixes the motor horizon to **16**, not the source proposal's 32 commands, and supplies future labels only over those 16 slots. The model therefore predicts four local ordered keyframes and revises them in receding horizon; it does not predict a full red-to-blue route from one observation. Only eight motor commands are executed per framework invocation. Training slots are t-1 through t+14, with future observations after each action. No longer-horizon labels or future deployment observations are invented.
2. No coarse sampling hook is provided by the outer sampler. The model therefore contains a small auxiliary **task-space diffusion model** with an explicitly defined 100-step cosine noising schedule and six-step deterministic DDIM inference. Its fixed Gaussian starting latent is a model buffer, saved and reloaded with EMA. Its route is a repeatable conditional sample, recomputed from the current history on every call, rather than fresh random geometry on every motor DDPM step. This auxiliary route sampler does not change the framework's 100-step DDPM motor training or sampling. There is no persistent cached plan or hidden episode clock. Additional inference cost is six small route-network evaluations per motor denoising evaluation.
3. A hard active-object identity is not supplied. A learned two-way soft reference head forms a convex red/blue position reference. A weak training target favors whichever object is nearer the labeled future TCP over the local window. This is **local geometric relevance**, not a ground-truth contact/active-object classifier. At initial or midway configurations it can be ambiguous. Both object poses, their relative geometry and goals remain in the fine context, so choosing this coordinate origin does not prescribe a manipulation phase.
4. Ordered keyframe timing is represented by a learned within-quartile offset, not an unconstrained long-duration plan. During training, event-weighted pooling in each four-slot bin emphasizes opening/closure, crossing upward through 0.26 m and vertical reversal; every valid slot still has nonzero weight. Predicted offsets decode to times in [0,3], [4,7], [8,11], [12,15], so order is structural. Differences between these times represent local inter-keyframe intervals in observation slots. We do not claim exact physical event timing or an external scheduler.
5. TCP rotation is represented by the first two columns of the rotation matrix computed from normalized wxyz quaternions. This avoids quaternion sign ambiguity in labels, but the network's six-component predictions are **not** projected onto SO(3), nor used to claim an orientation constraint. The fine motor decoder learns demonstrated orientations from actions and states.

## Architecture and causal conditioning

`build_model` constructs a 256-dimensional observation encoder, the reference head, the small residual MLP route epsilon denoiser, a five-channel-per-waypoint phase head, the public conditional U-Net motor epsilon backbone, and a temporal-convolution TCP trajectory predictor.

The causal encoder receives the two 47-dimensional raw observations after shared normalization, their normalized difference, and five XYZ relations for each history frame: TCP-red, TCP-blue, blue-red, red-red_goal and blue-blue_goal. These relations are divided by the **shared TCP full-range scales**, not refitted skill scales. World z, both orientations, absolute qpos/qvel, finger state and the goals remain available. This is not SE(3) invariance or action equivariance.

The coarse route has four eleven-channel tokens: reference-relative TCP XYZ, six rotation components, normalized gripper command, and within-bin temporal offset. XYZ uses the same shared full TCP ranges. The route network has three 256-wide residual MLP blocks with sinusoidal diffusion-step embeddings. Its internal alpha_bar is cos-squared with offset 0.008, normalized at time zero and floored at 1e-4. It is trained on independent random coarse noise/times. Internal inference uses steps 99,79,59,39,19,0 with eta zero. Coarse clean estimates are numerically clipped to [-2,2] for relative XYZ and [-1,1] for other channels; these are representation bounds, not physical safety constraints.

The fine U-Net receives a learned 256-dimensional condition made from observation context, generated route, predicted phase probabilities and reference weights. It has the prescribed widths 128/256/512 and predicts epsilon for the native eight-channel normalized action representation. Its direct qpos/qvel context is important: a task-space waypoint cannot by itself identify a feasible Panda joint route or account for PD tracking lag.

The nonrecurrent trajectory predictor consumes the complete encoded motor horizon, learned temporal position embeddings and causal observation context. Three residual convolutional blocks predict subsequent normalized TCP XYZ and rotation-6D, as residuals from the current observed TCP representation. This is a horizon-state surrogate, not a causal physics integrator within the action horizon; it can use adjacent planned actions. At deployment it is not an action controller. Future observations enter target construction only, never `model.forward`.

The decoder need not ignore contact corrections to fit an overly rigid waypoint skeleton: it retains the observation context and produces all 16 detailed joint/gripper targets. Conversely this bypass means a trained model might underuse the route; the representation and auxiliary consistency encourage but do not prove hierarchical usage.

## Losses and gradient paths

All losses are normalized by their valid target counts. `mask` and `future_mask` are intersected for state/coarse losses. Waypoint masks require at least one valid future/action slot in their bin. Padding does not cross a skill boundary and is never a target. High-noise decoded-action auxiliaries are weighted by the supplied motor alpha_bar, and the reconstructed clean actions are clipped to [-1.5,1.5] only inside these losses. The official sampler still uses its fixed clipping rule.

The total is motor epsilon loss plus the following weighted prior terms:

- **0.40 coarse epsilon MSE:** noise on the pooled 4x11 route labels is predicted by the trainable route network conditioned on the causal encoder. This is a genuine task-space denoising diffusion objective.
- **0.25 sampled-route MSE:** the actual deterministic inference route is supervised against those same labels, with gradients through its six denoising revisions. This addresses the fixed-seed inference adaptation and couples training to the route actually seen by the fine decoder.
- **0.15 trajectory supervision:** sum of masked TCP/orientation losses using demonstrated actions and motor x0 predictions as trajectory-predictor input. The decoded branch is alpha_bar weighted. The demonstrated-action branch learns the action/state surrogate; the decoded branch backpropagates to both the surrogate and motor epsilon prediction. The surrogate is learned from demonstrations, not an exact FK map.
- **0.10 route/motor consistency:** linearly interpolate predicted dense TCP states at the generated within-bin times and compare with the generated route expressed in shared normalized world coordinates. An additional quarter-weight gripper-consistency term compares interpolated decoded gripper with the route gripper. Validity is interpolated and detached. Gradients reach route coordinates/timing, soft reference, trajectory predictor and the decoded motor actions. Alpha_bar limits high-noise amplification.
- **0.03 phase BCE:** five trainable multi-label outputs predict rising, descending, high lateral transit, opening and closing. Labels are future-derived local motion/gripper events, pooled using keyframe weights. The probabilities also condition motor denoising. They are not deployment inputs and not certified contact states.
- **0.01 soft-reference cross entropy:** trainable object-reference logits are supervised by a softmax of local future TCP-object squared distances with a fixed 0.01 square-metre temperature. This metric constant is not a fitted normalizer. Route labels use a detached predicted anchor so the loss cannot be minimized merely by shifting that target origin.
- **0.02 low-height lateral-sweep penalty:** predicted decoded-trajectory XY increments beyond 0.0015 m per observation slot incur squared cost divided by (0.01 m)^2, gated smoothly by predicted height around 0.12 m with 0.025 m width. It is alpha_bar weighted. A training-only exemption covers demonstrated TCP XY within 0.04 m of an object whose center is below 0.04 m, if the demonstrated gripper is open or TCP z is below 0.065 m. This permissively approximates aligned approach/supported placement. It is not ground-truth support detection. Because the penalized increments and heights are **trainable predictions**, this is not a constant observed-pose penalty. Supervised trajectory losses counter the temptation to evade it by hallucinating height.

The main motor epsilon objective also differentiates through the generated route and phase conditioning. No loss solely on observed geometry is reported as a training prior. No controller chooses joints, waypoints, gripper switches or phase from demonstration indices.

## Shared normalization and fixed budget

Observation `(raw - mean)/std` uses the supplied complete-original-demonstration normalizer unchanged; here `std` is the limits-mode half range, with the supplied floors and quaternion unit scales. Relative XYZ uses twice the corresponding shared TCP half range. All actions remain in the repository's affine complete-demo encoding. There is no tiny per-skill fit, learned rescaling of raw units or clipping of observation inputs. Constant drawer channels are compatibility inputs only; this task has no drawer.

The outer training remains seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW learning rate 1e-4 and decay 1e-6, 500-step warmup/cosine schedule, EMA 0.999, 100 motor DDPM steps and last-EMA selection. No feedback-driven tuning or task-performance selection is used. Dependencies are Torch, math and `appl.public`; no filesystem/network, images, simulator kinematics or extra labels are needed inside the submitted implementation.

## Handoff, applicability and selection guidance

Select this policy for uncluttered upright block transfers with downward TCP and states corresponding to the full expanded red-buffer/blue-acquisition transition. Home starts are supported, but the model is not reset to home when invoked during overlap. Absolute TCP height, qpos/qvel, finger opening, current object/TCP relation, and two-frame motion identify whether it is descending with red, opening over supported red, retreating, approaching blue, or lifting blue. Different object locations distinguish initially open high home from the later open high retreat/blue approach. Object co-motion plus a narrow finger gap is evidence compatible with attachment, not proof of a secure grasp.

Continue while the intended shared transition is unfinished and no deliberate successor handoff has been selected. Red being outside its old region is insufficient to declare acquisition readiness. A nominal useful exit has supported released red and closed-grip blue with upward co-motion, as at the measured ends; do not demand a stationary dwell or completion of a stored coarse plan. The successor may take over earlier in its demonstrated overlap, including red descent, if given the actual current history. It should receive updated TCP/object poses, qpos/qvel and fingers and continue the unfinished motion from those observations. See HANDOFF.json for concrete evidence and warnings.

Final task success is exclusively the supplied completion contract: both red and blue must satisfy the rotated-XY containment and z conditions at their swapped goals. This buffer-red skill alone does not finish the exchange. A gripper release, clearance, velocity bound or extra dwell is not added to the task-success predicate; skill-readiness cues are separate.

## Limitations and untested claims

The 0.27-0.30 m arcs may be a planner template, not necessary clearance. Four local keyframes can blur event details, weighted pooling is not exact event detection, and the fixed-seed route is less expressive than a freely sampled multimodal plan. Independent coarse noise and the motor DDPM are not a single joint generative model. Internal diffusion adds compute. The trajectory surrogate may rely on context instead of action effects and can be wrong off-demonstration. Consistency is agreement between learned predictions, not a physical certificate. There is no hard collision avoidance, Panda joint-limit projection, pose constraint, supported-state guarantee, contact-belief GRU, IK, occupancy planner or certified disturbance recovery.

Low-table lateral motion, descent while misaligned, joint-limit saturation, trajectory/route disagreement or failure of a cube to follow the TCP indicate potential hierarchy failure; they are not empirically validated triggers for autonomous recovery. Compare against flat joint DP and a hierarchy without height/order losses at matched sampling compute to test the hypothesis. This session supplies interface/trainability validation only, not evidence of better success or safer execution.

# Grasp-frame-conditioned diffusion for finish_red__h03

## Assigned research hypothesis (unchanged)

> Compensate object goals in the current grasp frame: represent the target TCP pose through the measured object-to-tool transform instead of treating the TCP as the object center. Hypothesis: an object-relative diffusion representation improves precise final placement under small grasp-offset changes.

This is heuristic 3, candidate F, for the M1_v2 expanded **finish_red** skill. The implementation below is an executable adaptation, not an amendment to the supplied heuristic. It is a learned action diffusion model throughout. The proposed robustness advantage is a hypothesis, not a measured result from this session.

## Data, full-slice coverage and handoff

Training uses every action in all twelve assigned expanded ranges, with the repository's boundary masks. There is no phase-based filtering, new segmentation, replay index, demonstration clock or hand-coded joint trajectory. Ranges are recorded verbatim in pipeline.json. The initial overlap with transfer_blue is approximately 239–250 actions per demonstration, not just a red-placement segment. For example demo10100 has overlap [600,850) and assigned actions [600,1064); demo10111 has overlap [603,844) and actions [603,1061). Their final states at 1064 and 1061 are supported terminal labels, not extra actions.

The learned behavior therefore includes:

1. Finishing blue descent, support and opening at blue_goal while red remains in the buffer.
2. Empty withdrawal/transit to red, open-finger descent, closure and lift.
3. Red transport and grasp-compensated final setdown while leaving blue in place.
4. Demonstrated final opening, object settling and empty TCP retreat.

Actual observations resolve the frame and action distinctions:

- demo10100:600 has blue at approximately [-0.35579,-0.20004,0.20930] m and TCP [-0.36444,-0.19983,0.20950] m, with fingers about 0.01827/0.01824 m and gripper command -1. Early entry is still a blue-held transition, not an empty red approach. The measured start distribution spans TCP heights about 0.138–0.209 m and summed finger widths about 0.03651 m.
- At :630 blue is nearly supported; at :640 it is at z about 0.020 m, the fingers have opened to about 0.03822 m and the command is +1. Geometry alone can already satisfy the blue predicate before this release motion has finished.
- At :755 fingers are open at 0.04 m and TCP is about [-0.18719,0.00725,0.26523] m over red. At :800 and :810 the TCP is low and close to red but fingers are still open. Proximity is not evidence of a settled attachment.
- At :820 fingers have closed near 0.01825 m. The adjacent :839/:840 and :849/:850 pairs show TCP and red moving together during lift. The red-minus-TCP x displacement is about +0.00845 m, with y/z discrepancies much smaller. At :850 and :960 that offset persists despite very different absolute arm configurations.
- Independently, demo10111:844 and :966 show a red x offset about +0.00846 m. At :966 red is [-0.35095,0.19525,0.02431] m while TCP is [-0.35941,0.19542,0.02433] m. A TCP placed at the block-goal center would ignore this attachment geometry.
- demo10111:978 and demo10100:979/:980 show opening and supported red; the red/TCP relation must no longer be treated as rigid during retreat. Final TCP heights are approximately 0.3003 m, fingers approximately 0.04 m each, with both objects near their goals. The measured end distribution is narrow, but those values are evidence, not hard applicability thresholds.

HANDOFF.json supplies semantic entry/exit guidance. Two causal observations provide qpos/qvel, finger positions/velocities, world TCP and object poses and fixed world goal axes. Their differences distinguish descent from lift/retreat and expose object–TCP co-motion. These cues let the model take over during old-blue attachment, empty transit, new-red attachment or terminal opening without an externally initialized phase counter or cached transform. A stationary closed pair is still ambiguous; inferred attachment is not measured contact. The model does not claim arbitrary recovery from failed grasps.

## Representation and normalization

All raw poses are world-frame metres with wxyz quaternions. The shared normalizer is the one fitted on the complete twelve original demonstrations (12,742 samples, normalizer hash fc51579717dc36a089ba2d22956960d65828acd5bcb57e93d6362641102d37d1). Registered buffers use its observation mean and HALF-RANGE scales. Quaternion input components have the shared unit bounds; there is no observation clipping and no slice-scale refit. Native actions remain the supplied affine-normalized seven absolute Panda joint targets plus increasing-toward-open gripper command. The sampler performs the inverse action normalization and its prescribed bounds; this package has no additional action controller.

For both objects and both history frames, construct

- D = inverse(T_tcp) T_object, using unit quaternions and tool-frame translation d.
- A goal object transform with the observed object's current yaw, zero roll/pitch and the supplied world goal position. This preserves world gravity and goal-square axes; it is a near-upright representation choice, not a required goal quaternion.
- T_tcp_star = T_object_goal inverse(D). In coordinates, R_star = R_goal R_D^T and p_star = goal - R_star d.
- The actual local SE(3) error log(inverse(T_tcp) T_tcp_star), using a shortest-sign SO(3) quaternion logarithm and the inverse left Jacobian for its translation component.

Each object's 24 features contain local attachment translation, six entries from the first two columns of R_D, world object-to-goal displacement, absolute p_star in shared TCP normalization, world TCP-to-p_star error, and local SE(3) log translation/rotation. Relative translations use one isotropic length scale: the maximum of the **shared full-demo** TCP/red/blue xyz half-ranges, approximately 0.21313 m. This allows rotation of local vectors without introducing a new tiny per-skill coordinate scale. Rotation logs use pi as an analytic angular scale. Absolute world coordinates and qpos/qvel remain available: this is not global SE(3) invariance or equivariance.

A learned MLP (238 to 128 to 64 to 3) uses the two normalized observations, both objects' transforms and their one-step feature differences to estimate probabilities in the order blue-attached, empty, red-attached. Blue and red geometric blocks are independently probability-gated, not merged into one persistent calibration. An 18-feature empty block per frame supplies TCP-to-red world/local geometry, red-to-goal displacement, absolute TCP position and world orientation axes. It is gated by the empty probability. Thus the model can learn open approach and open retreat without interpreting either as an attached-object goal correction. All transforms and probabilities are recomputed from the current causal history at each denoising call; there is no retained blue offset to initialize a later red grasp. Probabilistic gating is learned, not an exact guarantee of zero attachment confidence when open.

The 276-dimensional conditioning vector concatenates raw normalized history, these gated blocks, role probabilities and normalized observation differences. A trainable 276 to 256 to 256 encoder with SiLU/LayerNorm produces the global diffusion condition. The absolute-observation path deliberately preserves robot configuration and gives the learned policy enough information for the entire transition, including valid-goal retreat. It can also allow the network to underuse the prior, an ablation-relevant limitation.

## Diffusion and learned decoder: explicit interface adaptation

The framework fixes action-space noising and requires epsilon in shape [B,16,8]. Consequently this implementation does **not** replace the sampler with a Cartesian/SE(3) latent diffusion process. It adapts the candidate's task-space-latent suggestion into grasp-relative conditioning of an action-space diffusion model plus a learned state-conditioned decoder. The common conditional U-Net has widths 128/256/512, kernel 5, groups 8 and timestep embedding 128. Its eight output channels are an unconstrained action-noise proposal, not claimed to be metrically calibrated Cartesian latent coordinates.

A per-slot residual joint-noise decoder, with hidden layers 128/128, takes that proposal, the noisy eight-action vector, normalized current qpos/qvel, a learned 64-dimensional geometric context and a 16-dimensional sinusoidal diffusion-time embedding. Its eight outputs are added to the proposal. The final layer starts at zero to retain an ordinary conditional-DP starting point, but all decoder layers are trainable. Through the fixed DDPM clean estimate this decodes to the shared-normalized **absolute joint/gripper** action coordinates, not realized qpos or Cartesian motor commands. There is no external IK, analytic FK, Jacobian service, optimization-based controller, or separate post-sampling decoder.

A training-only action-conditioned pose predictor supplies a kinematics-aware *learned* coupling: a 128-channel causal convolutional network with dilations 1,2,4,8, conditioning on current normalized qpos/qvel, the same learned 64-dimensional context, and a within-chunk slot coordinate. This slot coordinate is just position inside the horizon, never a source trajectory index or global progress signal. It predicts eighteen quantities: world displacements of TCP/red/blue from their current positions, and the three local relative rotation vectors for those bodies. Translations and rotations use the same length/pi scales as above. This is a finite-horizon learned dynamics surrogate, not rigid-body FK, contact physics, an inverse solver, or recurrent progress memory. It is trained on realized subsequent observations and can learn controller tracking lag because absolute joint targets are not mistaken for measured next qpos.

## Losses and gradient paths

Let m be the action mask and f its intersection with future_mask. All futures are labels only. Slot j is used exactly as supplied: action t-1+j and its subsequent observation. In particular slot zero can target the current state; no extra one-step shift is introduced. No target crosses a skill boundary.

1. **Diffusion:** masked mean squared epsilon error, using the public epsilon_loss. This trains the U-Net, joint-noise decoder, conditioning encoder and attachment probabilities end-to-end on every phase.
2. **Weak attachment supervision:** soft cross entropy on the role logits. A causal target uses the current mean per-finger position, TCP/object distance, and disagreement of one-step object/TCP world displacements. The closed score ramps between demonstrated open 0.04 m and held 0.01825 m. The distance kernel uses the physical block half-size 0.02 m, and co-motion uses a 0.002 m numerical floor plus observed motion energy. Blue/red scores are normalized if necessary, remaining probability is empty, with 1% uniform smoothing. These are physical weak-label bandwidths, not fitted feature normalizers, contact measurements, action rules or success thresholds. Stationary proximity can still mislabel pre-contact. The targets are detached, but cross entropy depends on trainable logits and therefore has a real gradient.
3. **Teacher-action future-pose loss:** feed ground-truth shared-normalized actions to the pose predictor and compare its trainable outputs with actual future displacement/rotation labels. Use masked Smooth L1 with beta 0.1 in the scaled pose coordinates. This anchors the dynamics surrogate on demonstration actions; it trains the predictor, context/condition encoders and role gating.
4. **Denoised-action future-pose loss:** compute x0 = (x_t - sqrt(1-alpha_bar) epsilon_hat)/sqrt(alpha_bar). Feed x0 clamped to [-2,2] to the same predictor and compare against the same masked future labels. Each sample is weighted by alpha_bar without re-normalizing away its attenuation. This bounds outliers and reduces high-noise amplification. The bound is auxiliary-only; deployment epsilon and the supplied sampling procedure are unchanged. Gradients flow through predicted poses, the predicted clean actions and the decoder into the U-Net, as well as into conditioning and the surrogate. Clamped-out coordinates have no direct auxiliary action gradient but retain the main epsilon gradient.

Total loss = diffusion_loss + 0.02 attachment_CE + 0.10 teacher_pose_loss + 0.05 denoised_pose_loss. prior_loss is exactly the latter three weighted terms. Each pose term divides by the count of valid pose scalar coordinates (18 per valid slot). There are no observed-pose-only penalties masquerading as training objectives, contract-margin penalties, projected grasp constraints, global success reward, or unimplemented consistency terms. Jointly trained pose predictions are a soft supervised regularizer; they cannot certify a correct grasp or physical feasibility.

## Deployment, continuation and completion

Forward receives only noisy actions, diffusion timestep and [B,2,47] causal history. It never reads future_obs, demonstration identity, source time, future role labels or hidden simulator data. Pose prediction and weak-label construction run only in compute_loss. Default execution is the framework's 100-step DDPM over horizon 16 followed by eight executed actions, with fresh observations for subsequent invocations. AdamW, EMA, normalization, clipping and execution accounting remain the repository's responsibility. The declared final budget is seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 1e-6, 500 warmup steps, cosine decay and EMA 0.999; select the last EMA, not validation-tuned results.

The API should continue this policy through supported blue release/empty transit/red manipulation if those phases remain. Once red and blue meet the declared contract, task success does not require finger opening, clearance, an exact zero goal error, low speed, or a sustained hold. The contract uses world goal centers, 0.06 m goal-square half extents, 0.02 m object half-size, full rotated XY containment and 0.011 m z tolerance. If execution continues for orderly withdrawal, the data support open-finger retreat to about z=0.30 m; this is retained behavior rather than a new success predicate. Pass measured poses, goals and causal qpos/qvel/finger history to any successor, not an internal grasp transform. A geometric goal may become true while release/retreat is still in progress; distinguish task completion from optional continuation readiness.

## Dependencies, limitations and prospective tests

Dependencies are only torch, math and the supplied appl.public backbone/loss helper. No image encoder, external package, file access, IK service or replay is used. Fixed-base joint configuration, gravity and world axes remain explicit. The approximately 8.45 mm offsets and near-identity block orientations vary little across these demonstrations, so a constant-offset or ordinary world-state DP could perform just as well. Two-frame attachment inference can confuse closed-but-unattached contact, slips, stationary support and closure transients. The decoder/predictor may not generalize to unseen arm configurations, buffer positions, yaw ranges or new grasp offsets. The model has no hard mechanism preventing blue disturbance, regrasp after success, collision, or invalid release. Its continuation cues are learned, not certified.

Prospective experiments, not executed here: compare against equal-capacity raw-world joint DP, remove grasp-compensated target features, replace observed transforms with a constant per-object offset, remove weak-role or future-pose losses, and validate modest offset/yaw perturbations and mid-overlap handoffs. Package interface checks establish trainability, sampling and reload compatibility only; they do not measure task performance or support claims of robustness.

# Occupancy-conditioned staging graph — buffer_red__h01

## Assigned scientific hypothesis

The assigned heuristic is the **occupancy-conditioned staging graph**: explicitly represent which distinguishable object occupies which goal, predict a free intermediate site, and condition learned motor diffusion on that relational representation. Its hypothesis is reduced destination confusion and greater tolerance to modest starting-position variation compared with a flat world-state diffusion policy. This implementation retains that identity and the original segmentation. This document records implementation choices, not a modification of the source heuristic.

The task exchanges red and blue between two marked regions. Red's goal is initially occupied by **blue**. Merely seeing an object near red_goal must not imply red success. The first skill must move red out of the initially red region, leave it supported at a free buffer, and continue into blue acquisition. Only red-first solutions and one buffer site are observed; deliberate free-space reasoning cannot be distinguished from memorization of a staging waypoint here.

## Causal representation and learned architecture

Deployment receives exactly two causal 47-component raw observations. All positions are world metres, orientations wxyz quaternions, arm joints radians, fingers metres, and velocities in the supplied native units. There are no images, prior commands, timestamps, episode IDs, stored initial snapshots or future states in forward. Fixed-zero drawer compatibility channels are unused. The public shared observation/action normalizer is unchanged. The robot branch uses the shared scales for qpos, qvel and TCP pose. All learned geometric vectors, including relative XYZ, use the same complete-demonstration TCP XYZ half-ranges; in particular, relative x is not divided by blue's narrow empirical x range. Nothing is refitted on this skill.

Five ordered, identity-labelled graph nodes represent TCP, red, blue, red_goal and blue_goal. Each frame's node features comprise world XYZ normalized with the common TCP origin/scales, quaternion, five-way identity, the two-frame displacement, projected XY half-extents, and height relative to the goal center height: 18 features total. The displacement is an inter-observation difference, not a claimed time-calibrated Cartesian velocity. Cube projections use the absolute first two rows of the wxyz rotation matrix times the 0.02 m cube half-side. Goals have XY half-extents 0.06 m; TCP is a point for these features.

Every directed edge has normalized relative XYZ, normalized distance, two receiver-inside-sender containment margins, two footprint-overlap margins, and relative inter-frame displacement (11 features). Thus TCP-object offsets and object-goal occupancy are explicit and color identities are not pooled away. Two shared learned attention/message-passing rounds operate at width 64. These are representations of geometry, not externally commanded actions or hard constraints.

An absolute robot/world MLP maps the two qpos/qvel/TCP frames (50 values) to 128 features. The complete two-frame graph and robot features predict five role logits. Their **soft probabilities**, not a thresholded state machine, modulate graph nodes through a learned role embedding and FiLM layer. A learned readout produces 192 role-conditioned graph features. The roles are approach_red, carry_red, buffer_release, approach_blue, and blue_lift; the latter includes blue closure/acquisition immediately preceding lift.

### Executable staging adaptation

The original proposal suggests predicting a site and uncertainty from the initial occupancy graph with the complete demonstration's first released-red pose as label. The supplied model interface has only two causal states and a 16-action future window. It neither supplies an initial-state cache at every invocation nor a full-trajectory first-release label. We therefore recompute a **blue-plus-two-goals blocker/layout subgraph** each call, using the same node encoder/message modules, and predict a diagonal Gaussian staging site from its temporally averaged embedding. Red and TCP still appear in the full action/role graph, but not in this layout head. This prevents trivial copying of the current supported-red pose into the staging prediction and makes the locally supervised layout largely identical before and after red release. It also permits invocation partway through the overlap without an initial-state memory. This deliberate factorization assumes the demonstrated red-first dependency; it is not general object-order planning.

The head emits a mean in shared TCP-normalized world coordinates and three uncertainties, sigma = 0.05 + 0.95 sigmoid(logit) in those coordinates. This lower bound is a chosen regularizer in the existing scale system, not a per-skill fitted scale or measured contact tolerance. No buffer waypoint is hard-coded in the model, and no target is computed from source indices. The proposal is trained by valid local future labels whenever they are available, together with soft free-site penalties throughout the slice. Early-state supervision transfers through the shared blocker layout; direct initial-to-distant-release supervision is **not** claimed.

The final 352-dimensional diffusion condition concatenates graph summary (192), robot features (128), role probabilities (5), predicted normalized staging mean and log sigma (6), offsets from the predicted site to all five current nodes (15), predicted site/goal overlap margins (4), and site/blue overlap margins (2). Predicted quantities are used in **every** training and inference call, with no teacher forcing or oracle substitution. Site uncertainty is conditioning, not a calibrated safety gate.

The public Conditional U-Net predicts epsilon for [B,16,8] shared-normalized absolute [seven joint targets, gripper command] chunks. Widths are 128/256/512, timestep embedding 128, kernel 5, groups 8. World/robot information is deliberately retained: this model does not make joint actions translation- or rotation-equivariant. The fixed DDPM sampler and framework handle noising, 100-step denoising, clipping, native decoding, bounds and eight-step execution; no IK, FK, action script, extra sampler correction, or replay is implemented.

## Training losses and gradient paths

The total loss is

`L = L_epsilon + 0.05 L_site_NLL + 0.10 L_role_CE + 0.02 L_free_site`.

1. **Denoising:** public mask-normalized epsilon MSE over valid action slots and all eight channels. This trains the U-Net, full graph, robot branch, role probabilities and staging proposal through their differentiable conditioning paths. Training and deployment call the same predict_with_aux computation; forward returns epsilon only.
2. **Staging likelihood:** in future_obs, select valid supported-buffer candidates: red center height within 0.006 m of the goal center height, its projected XY bounding box outside both marked regions, less than 0.003 m motion from the preceding valid state, and either measured total finger width above 0.06 m or TCP-red distance above 0.06 m. The first future slot is the state after action t-1, namely the current state, so its predecessor comparison uses the current causal observation; following slots compare adjacent future states. The target is the mean red XYZ over candidates in the window, converted using shared TCP scales. A sample with no candidates contributes zero likelihood. The per-axis likelihood is `0.5*((mean-target)/sigma)^2 + log(sigma/0.05)`, averaged over axes and valid samples. The logarithmic constant merely shifts Gaussian NLL to a nonnegative minimum. This loss trains both site mean and uncertainty plus shared layout graph modules. It cannot supervise a distant release unavailable within the future window.
3. **Role cross-entropy:** weak training-only labels use current object height, TCP proximity, free-region geometry, two-frame red descent, measured finger width, and the current native gripper target (slot 1, since slots span t-1 through t+14). Current closure commands are labels, never inference inputs. Red near TCP with closing fingers or elevated red is carry_red; free-region red descending near TCP, or low red near TCP, is buffer_release; supported buffered red with TCP farther than 0.10 m becomes approach_blue; blue within 0.055 m of TCP with measured or commanded closure becomes blue_lift/acquisition. Later-phase rules override earlier ones. The default is approach_red. These geometric thresholds annotate observed demonstrations; forward does not execute these rules or constrain actions with them. The cross-entropy trains the role head and causal encoder. Ambiguous dwelling/contact boundaries remain noisy labels.
4. **Predicted free-site penalty:** squared positive minimum-axis overlap of the *predicted* staging footprint with either 0.06 m half-width region, plus overlap with blue's current projected footprint, plus squared predicted center-height error relative to goal center height. Margins are divided by shared horizontal scales, and height error by the shared TCP z scale. The hypothetical site's footprint uses current red orientation as a proxy; the model does not predict future red orientation. These losses differentiate through staging XYZ, not through an observed-pose-only expression. They encourage a supported-height proposal clear of both regions and blue in projection. They do not certify support, arm reachability or collision-free swept motion.

All target construction is under no_grad; predicted roles/sites and conditioning are not detached. Future_mask governs staging supervision and action mask governs diffusion. No auxiliary uses a reconstructed clean action at high-noise timesteps, avoiding amplified x0 errors. There is no action/state-dynamics or kinematic model beyond the learned diffusion itself.

Training is the supplied fixed 20,000-update, batch-128, seed-0 AdamW recipe at 1e-4 learning rate and 1e-6 weight decay, 500-step warmup, cosine schedule, gradient norm 1, EMA 0.999, and final EMA selection. No performance-dependent budget or checkpoint selection is introduced.

## Handoff, selection and termination

Use this policy from the demonstrated open home state or from supported intermediate states in the expanded overlap. Initially, identity-labelled blue-to-red_goal edges show the blocked destination. During red descent, low finger width, red/TCP relative offset and descending co-motion distinguish held red from an already supported red buffer. After opening, unchanged low red pose while TCP rises distinguishes retreat from red still following the hand. Supported red plus a TCP moving toward blue changes role within the overlap, not at its endpoint. Near blue, finger closure is useful but insufficient; blue rising with TCP at approximately fixed offset is stronger acquisition evidence. qvel and two causal pose frames provide direction of motion without an elapsed-time schedule.

The model learns the entire local sequence: red approach, pickup, carry, setdown, opening, retreat to roughly TCP z=0.30 m, blue approach, closure, and initial blue lift. If red is only low and centered but remains held, continuing this skill to release/retreat is appropriate. The successor may also take over in this shared transition because transfer_blue has matching observed support. A useful later exit is red left supported outside both regions and blue lifting under closed grip. No home reset is needed. Send actual causal qpos/qvel, TCP/object poses and goals to the successor; the predicted role/site is internal and is not exposed as a required cross-policy context channel by this interface.

These local handoff cues do not redefine the completion contract: final task success is red_at_goal AND blue_at_goal, with full rotated-XY containment and the supplied height tolerance. This first skill normally does not complete that exchange. Extra release, clearance, speed or sustained-hold conditions are not required task predicates.

## Applicability, scientific limitations and comparisons

Only small planar perturbations, distinguishable equal-sized blocks, the same Panda/table geometry and the demonstrated red-first order are supported. Arbitrary initial arm poses and contact-error recovery are unobserved. A free predicted buffer is not proof that it is reachable or that a released object will be supported there. A single site and narrow layouts permit the staging head to converge to a constant, and learned uncertainty may be uncalibrated. Its omission of red/TCP in site prediction helps sparse supervision transfer but cannot adapt the buffer to novel arm obstructions or red-dependent reachability. The footprint penalties are soft axis-aligned projected-box approximations, not exact future collision constraints.

There is no implemented free-space search, invariance guarantee, visual encoder, force sensing, IK, FK or certified grasp test. The scientifically relevant future comparisons are matched-capacity flat world-state DP and fixed-buffer conditioning, evaluating held-out initial-position sensitivity, destination confusion, occupied-region staging, failed release and failed blue co-lift. No such performance results are available during this implementation session. Interface smoke checks establish executability, finite training and reload behavior only, not task success.

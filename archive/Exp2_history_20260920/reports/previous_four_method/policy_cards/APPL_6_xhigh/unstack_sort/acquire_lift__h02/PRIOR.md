# acquire_lift__h02: causal contact-belief Diffusion Policy

## Assigned scientific hypothesis

The assigned heuristic is **infer manipulation state from causal coupling rather than the last gripper command or elapsed time**. A history-conditioned latent attachment belief should distinguish closing, holding and releasing, reducing premature lift and previous-to-next-object switching. This document records implementation decisions and limitations; it does not alter the source heuristic. This remains a learned action diffusion policy, not a controller built from phase rules.

The policy covers the **full expanded acquire_lift slice**. Its red invocation includes open approach, closing, lifting and initial lateral carry. Its blue invocation can start while red is still held above its pad, so training also includes red descent, release, open retreat/clearance, movement to blue, blue approach, closing, lift and initial carry. No source indices, segment progress, phase filtering, fixed skill schedule or hand-authored joint trajectories enter the model. All 24 assigned segments retain their boundaries and overlaps.

## Necessary interface adaptations

1. The fixed forward interface supplies only two 47D causal states, a 0.05 s baseline at 20 Hz. The proposed twelve-observation/0.6 s rolling history cannot be implemented without changing that interface. We use a two-step GRU plus observed qvel and explicit one-step feature differences. There is no long-horizon recurrent memory across calls and no hidden history introduced during sampling.
2. Neither invocation role tags nor previous action commands are input fields. The five proposed selected/previous role states are therefore represented by six **physical object-indexed modes**: empty approach/retreat, red closing, red attached/carrying, red releasing, blue closing and blue attached/carrying. Red-held may represent selected red on its first invocation or previous red at blue entry; the observation geometry resolves what continuation was demonstrated. Red pose relative to its pad and to blue, both TCP-object relations, measured finger positions and qvel provide causal evidence. This is not an explicit semantic task-selector and does not support arbitrary role orders.
3. The duration-aware semi-Markov proposal is reduced to soft **one-step persistence**: a learned transition matrix initialized to favor self transitions and a small label-aware adjacent-belief consistency term. No elapsed manipulation time or learned duration counter exists. The DDPM noise timestep is still supplied to the denoiser, as required, but is not episode time.
4. No history mask is supplied. The first recurrent step has zero differences. The second has actual feature differences and an exact-duplicate informativeness flag; a repeated initial observation contributes no motion evidence. This is not a reliable distinction between padding and a genuinely stationary hand. The recurrent hidden vector starts at zero, but its belief is inferred from the first available physical observation, **not** initialized to the empty category. A warm pair at a skill boundary is preferred.
5. All future labels are proxies from successful demonstrations. Relative quaternion-change regression supplements relative-translation persistence; it is not a measured rigid contact constraint.

## Representation, normalization and learned architecture

Use `spec.normalizer`, fitted once on all complete original training demonstrations, unchanged. Observation normalization is `(raw-mean)/std`, where std is the common half-range. Actions remain the repository's shared affine normalization of seven absolute Panda joint targets plus one gripper command. There is no per-skill fit, rescaling of the action representation, clipping of observations or normalization learned from the small slice.

Each observation produces 67 features: all 47 normalized state channels; six world-frame TCP-to-object translation components; six goal-minus-object components; and two four-component TCP-relative quaternions. Relative translations use the axiswise maximum of the shared TCP/red/blue position half-ranges, not a narrow local difference scale. Relative quaternions use unit components, with sign chosen by the largest-magnitude component. They do not claim global invariance. The raw world-frame state path and absolute joint action space explicitly retain frame dependence.

For each frame, concatenate these features, a 67D causal difference and one informativeness bit (135 inputs). A 160-wide MLP feeds a 160-state GRUCell run twice. A shared emission MLP outputs six logits. Let p0 be the first emission softmax, T the row-softmax of a learned 6x6 transition matrix, and e1 the second emission. The current belief is

`p1 = softmax(e1 + 0.35 log(p0 T + epsilon))`.

Self-transition logits initialize at 3.0, preferred off-diagonals at -0.5 and other transitions at -3.0. All transitions remain possible and learnable. Preferred edges include empty-to-closing for either object, closing-to-held, red-held-to-releasing, releasing-to-empty and reverse/reclosing edges. There is no hard phase guard.

Concatenate h1, h1-h0, current 67D features, p1, and a learned 32D mode embedding averaged under p1. A 425-to-256-to-256 network yields the condition for the public Conditional U-Net. Its widths are 128/256/512, temporal kernel 5, groups 8 and DDPM step embedding 128. The U-Net predicts epsilon for all 16x8 action slots. The physical feature path avoids forcing all continuous control detail through six categories, but it also means the model could learn to underuse the belief; effectiveness needs ablation.

A separate learned 256-wide forecast head maps that same causal condition to 16x19 outcome predictions. It predicts two future finger positions, TCP/red/blue displacement vectors and two relative-quaternion changes. There is no future-observation or teacher action input to either encoder or forecast head. It forecasts expert continuation, not state dynamics conditional on an arbitrary candidate action.

## Actual losses and gradient paths

The total scalar objective is

`L = L_epsilon + .12 L_phase + .01 L_temporal + .002 L_transition + .10 L_finger + .10 L_motion + .08 L_relative + .02 L_rotation`.

- **Diffusion:** public mask-normalized epsilon MSE on unchanged noisy normalized actions. This trains the U-Net, its conditioning network, recurrent encoder, mode embeddings, emissions and transition belief. No scripted action output is added.
- **Soft phase labels:** under `no_grad`, form weak six-mode targets using TCP-object proximity (Gaussian bandwidth 0.022 m), total finger width (soft pinch around 0.046 m with 0.004 m sigmoid width), the corresponding demonstrated gripper command, future finger opening, future object upward displacement/elevation and TCP-object relative-translation persistence. Future persistence uses a 0.010 m uncertainty bandwidth. The first observation uses command slot 0 and future slots 0-7; the current observation uses command slot 1 and future slots 1-8. These span up to eight subsequent 20 Hz steps, masked at slice boundaries. Red proximity to its goal at low height softens red closing and adds release mass. Object elevation reference values 0.080 m for red and 0.040 m for blue reflect the different initial support heights. Scores get an additive 0.015 pseudocount and normalize to uncertain distributions. They are neither one-hot contact truth nor deployed tests. Cross-entropy against both trainable beliefs is weighted by the corresponding action-slot validity. It trains emissions, the shared encoder and T.
- **Temporal persistence:** the squared difference of the two predicted mode distributions is weighted by `1 - TV(two soft label distributions)` and valid adjacent action masks. This allows more change near a labeled transition. It trains belief predictions, not observed motion.
- **Transition preference:** penalize the learned T's mean probability mass on discouraged edges. This trains T; no edge is forbidden and no deterministic mode progression is imposed.
- **Finger forecast:** masked MSE of trainable future finger predictions against future qpos[7:9], using the shared observation normalizer.
- **Motion forecast:** masked MSE of predicted future TCP, red and blue xyz displacements, relative to the current observation and divided by the common positional feature scale.
- **Relative translation:** subtract predicted TCP displacement from each predicted object displacement and compare to the corresponding labeled relative displacement changes. This trains a future-following representation without incorrectly forcing relative pose to remain fixed during release or empty retreat.
- **Relative orientation:** regress future-minus-current TCP-relative quaternion components after sign alignment to the current relative quaternion. These changes use unit-component scales, not a fitted small angular scale. This small signal may contribute little for the nearly fixed rotations in these demonstrations.

All forecast losses use `future_mask`; slot 0 is explicitly excluded because it is the state after action t-1, namely the current state. Actions are indexed t-1 through t+14 and future states t through t+15, so forecast slots 1-15 are actual future outcomes. Padded outcomes do not cross a skill slice. Every auxiliary depends on trainable predictions or parameters. None is a constant penalty computed solely on observed trajectories. Future targets do not enter forward. Forecast auxiliaries update their head and the shared causal condition/belief; they do not directly backpropagate through the action U-Net or enforce consistency of a sampled action with predicted motion. The diffusion term trains that action branch. This avoids high-noise x0 amplification but is a deliberate weaker coupling than an action-conditioned learned dynamics constraint.

## Deployment and handoff

`forward(noisy_action, timestep, raw_history)` has no stateful side effects. It recomputes the same causal belief from the supplied two observations at each denoising step, then predicts action noise. Standard 100-step DDPM and the fixed shared action decoder produce actions; the outer executor uses the declared eight-step prefix and reobserves. Belief values and outcome heads are not a custom stopping controller or exported successor interface.

At original start, supply the available initial state and causal padding. At takeover, preferably supply two actual consecutive states, retaining qpos/qvel, world TCP/object poses and both goals. Do not reset finger measurements or pretend blue entry is empty. Blue-entry red-held/downward geometry differs strongly from blue approach: red is near its negative-y pad and coupled to the TCP, while blue is still near the central support location. During release, measured opening and TCP rising away from stationary red support empty retreat. During selected lift, matching TCP/object displacement and nearly persistent relative pose support attachment, even with nonzero finger qvel.

The full expanded demonstrations teach these transitions directly. Continue this policy when approach/closure is still incomplete or while it must finish red placement and clear before blue acquisition. Transfer to carry/place when selected-object following during lift/initial carry and measured pinch are evident. Typical training exit z and widths are useful references, not calibrated posterior cutoffs. The forward interface exports actions only; the successor must re-encode causal physical observations. Invocation role intent can be retained by the inference agent, but no latent or future-derived phase label should be passed as contact truth. See HANDOFF.json for operational evidence and failure signatures.

## Scope, dependencies and limitations

Implementation dependencies are Torch and the repository's `appl.public` DiffusionBackbone/epsilon_loss. The outer framework owns data loading, fixed DDPM noising/sampling, shared normalization, AdamW, clipping, EMA and selection. The declared final budget is seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, horizon 16, history 2, execution 8, 100 train/inference diffusion steps, learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 1e-6, EMA .999, cosine schedule with 500-step warmup and gradient norm 1. Last EMA is selected; there is no performance-dependent tuning or schedule.

No IK, FK, image encoder, contact sensor, collision checker, exact equivariance, hard geometric constraint, force threshold, slip recovery or calibrated attachment probability is claimed. Object following is only a proxy; common motion can be confounded with grasp. Finger qvel can remain about -0.020/+0.026 m/s in successful holds, so zero finger speed is deliberately not required. The model cannot remember an arbitrary earlier grasp if the latest two observations are ambiguous. Forecasts can average different continuations and successful-only data do not calibrate failure detection. A short-history latent prior can still recover an expert's state-correlated timer implicitly. Suggested falsification tests are a history-matched diffusion model without mode/outcome losses, history removal, an absolute-time baseline, and delayed-closure or slip interventions. None has been run here.

The task contract is unchanged: both blocks satisfy their world-frame goal predicates, including full rotated XY containment and z tolerance 0.011 m. Release, TCP clearance, low speed and sustained hold are not additional task-success conditions. Opening/clearance discussed here are the demonstrated transition motions needed before acquiring blue, and carry-readiness cues are skill handoff guidance only.

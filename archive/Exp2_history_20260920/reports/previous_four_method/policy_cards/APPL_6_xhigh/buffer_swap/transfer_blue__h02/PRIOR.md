# transfer_blue__h02: Role-conditioned noninterference

## Assigned research hypothesis (unchanged)

Heuristic 2 protects completed and buffered objects by distinguishing active manipulation from passive support. The hypothesis is that explicit preservation helps chained manipulation avoid collateral motion that invalidates earlier progress. The source proposes a role-aware interaction-graph diffusion policy, action-conditioned forward-model ensemble, passive-motion/orientation objectives, and conservative ranking of several sampled action chunks. It explicitly warns that successful noninterference demonstrations do not identify collision dynamics and that static predictions are not safety guarantees. This implementation does not modify that source hypothesis or its segmentation.

## Executable adaptation and scope

The policy is a learned epsilon-predicting action diffusion model throughout. The interface exposes model.forward and compute_loss, not a candidate sampling/ranking hook. Consequently **multiple-candidate sampling, imitation-likelihood ranking, online ensemble scoring and a retained no-guidance candidate are not implemented**. The deployable part of this hypothesis is a role-conditioned graph representation plus training-time amortization of preservation into the denoiser. A three-head forward ensemble supplies differentiable preservation, pose-consistency and conservative clearance penalties on the DDPM clean-action estimate. Ensemble disagreement and expert-prediction error reduce those penalties during training. Inference remains one unmodified 100-step DDPM sample, decoded and executed by the repository. This adaptation cannot test the proposed candidate-selection component; it tests the representation and learned preservation objective only. A future candidate-ranking ablation needs an inference interface extension, not an undocumented controller.

No motion phase is scripted. There are no waypoints, index lookup, replay, IK, analytical FK, image encoders, hard safety filters, color-fixed passive constraints or elapsed-time encodings. Graph geometry does not make absolute joint targets equivariant. There is no recurrent state or external baseline-pose cache: each call uses the currently measured poses as baselines, not remembered nominal buffer/goal placements.

## Learned representation

1. The 94-dimensional causal observation history is normalized using the supplied shared complete-original-demonstration mean and half ranges, unchanged, and embedded to 128 dimensions. No tiny per-skill scales are fitted. Drawer compatibility zeros are not interpreted as a fixture.
2. A 64-dimensional TCP node embeds both observations' qpos/qvel and TCP pose. Each 64-dimensional object node shares an encoder over its two normalized poses, TCP-relative positions at both observations, goal displacement, inter-object displacement, backward displacement, finger observations, object identity and height above supported center z=0.020 m. All additional positional features use the shared complete-demo TCP half-range vector std[18:21], rather than the very narrow blue-x range. Goals and object identity remain available; this is not claimed to be a permutation or rigid-motion invariant action map.
3. A trainable shared role classifier sees each object embedding and global history. It predicts three probabilities: supported/passive, closed-TCP-associated, and transition/uncertain. The latter is a residual class, not a calibrated uncertainty estimate. Cross-entropy weak supervision is supplemented by end-to-end diffusion gradients. The classifier is used at deployment, not the label rules.
4. Two rounds of directed messages connect all three nodes. Edge features include shared-scale relative displacement/norm and sender/receiver role probabilities. The message network has passive-sender and nonpassive-sender branches mixed continuously by predicted passive probability. Incoming messages and residual node updates form a learned interaction representation. The TCP node is typed as the manipulator; object roles remain inferred. Flattened nodes, roles and global history form a 256-dimensional condition.
5. The supplied Conditional U-Net (widths 128/256/512, kernel 5, groups 8, time embedding 128) predicts epsilon for [B,16,8] normalized absolute joint/gripper chunks. The original affine action normalization and repository DDPM sampling are retained.
6. Three independent MLP forward heads take the learned 256-dimensional condition and the whole encoded action chunk. They predict, for each slot, red/blue world pose residuals and a TCP position residual to current measurements. Position residuals use the same shared geometry scales. Object quaternions are normalized after adding 0.1 times the predicted residual to current quaternion. Each head uses two 256-wide SiLU hidden layers; independently initialized heads use 0.8 Bernoulli bootstrap sample masks when fitted. These are learned action-to-state predictors, not analytical robot dynamics or FK.

## Labels, losses and gradient paths

The fixed epsilon objective is masked mean squared noise error. All other losses involve trainable predictions. No penalty merely computed from observed poses is added to the training objective.

**Current role labels.** A closed-associated weak label requires observed TCP-to-object center distance below 0.045 m and total measured finger width below 0.060 m. A passive label requires center z within 0.008 m of 0.020 m, backward displacement below 0.002 m and no closed association. Everything else is transition/uncertain. These thresholds are conservative training label definitions, not hard applicability tests or a controller. In particular, closed-width association turns passive protection off before the object visibly rises, preventing a color-fixed frozen red during retrieval. Open fingers near the table may count as passive after release. Role cross-entropy trains the encoder and classifier; learned probabilities also feed the diffusion condition.

**Forward fit.** Expert encoded chunks predict future red/blue poses and TCP positions. Targets follow the interface exactly: action slots t-1 through t+14, future state after each action. Position MSE is normalized by the shared geometry scales; sign-invariant orientation error is `1-(q_pred dot q_target)^2`. Per slot, the loss is mean over objects of position MSE plus 0.25 orientation error, plus TCP position MSE. Each ensemble fit is masked by action validity, future validity and its bootstrap mask, then ensemble-averaged. This trains the world heads and the shared observation encoder. Future states never enter deployed forward.

**Preservation weights.** For every future slot and object, a training-only interval label requires current passive role, future support, no future closed association, displacement from the current pose below 0.003 m, and orientation error to baseline below 0.001. A cumulative product switches preservation off for the rest of the chunk after a failed interval condition. An object released or regrasped within a chunk is not incorrectly protected across the active part. If the current role is active, preservation stays off for that object throughout this chunk, including any later release; subsequent invocations can protect it. This is a conservative short-horizon baseline, not a claim that objects can never move.

**Denoised-action auxiliaries.** Compute the usual x0 estimate from noisy actions and predicted epsilon, clamp to the sampler's [-1,1] clean-action range, and mask padded actions. Re-evaluate the forward ensemble with detached head parameters and detached conditioning, but differentiable x0 input. This blocks a shortcut that would train a fictitiously static world just to reduce the guidance loss. The gradient is prediction -> x0 -> epsilon -> U-Net/graph/role representation; expert forward fitting separately learns the head parameters. It can still fail if the learned head is action-insensitive, which cannot be excluded from success-only data.

- Passive preservation penalizes predicted object displacement from the current measured baseline, divided by the physical 0.020 m cube half-size, plus sign-invariant orientation change. The cube size is a physical tolerance, not a re-fitted action/observation normalizer.
- Future consistency penalizes predicted red/blue poses and TCP positions against their actual future labels, including active objects. It counterbalances simply freezing everything and provides action/state coupling.
- Clearance computes signed Euclidean distance of a predicted TCP point to the predicted oriented cube using inverse wxyz rotation and 0.020 m half-size. A squared hinge below 0.010 m is applied only to passive intervals when the currently observed TCP is over 0.050 m from that object and the demonstrated future point-to-cube distance exceeds 0.010 m. This deliberately does not penalize legitimate close release or grasp geometry. There is no test of finger, link, held-object or swept-volume collision.

All three denoised-action objectives have per-sample weight `alpha_bar * 1[alpha_bar > 0.25]`, leaving high-noise steps to epsilon fitting. Detached per-slot/per-object trust is `exp(-variance - calibration_error)`, where both are coordinate-mean squared position errors in units of 0.020 m: ensemble expert-action disagreement, and expert ensemble mean versus future label. This attenuates inaccurate training predictors, not a calibrated deployment confidence. Orientation disagreement is not used for trust. Each loss uses its validity/passive/trust mask in a normalized mean; the alpha factor attenuates the numerator rather than renormalizing it away.

The total loss is:

`L = L_epsilon + 0.05 L_role + 0.5 L_forward + 0.03 L_preserve + 0.005 L_clearance + 0.05 L_future_consistency`.

Future masks and action masks apply to all future losses, and no labels cross slice boundaries. The complete original-demo normalizer is a supplied buffer, never estimated in the model. Fixed training is seed 0, 20,000 updates, batch 128, AdamW 1e-4 with 1e-6 decay, gradient clipping 1, cosine schedule/500 warmup, EMA .999, horizon 16/history 2/execution 8, and 100 DDPM train/inference steps. Selection is the final EMA, not validation-based selection.

## Deployment and handoff

Only two raw causal observations, noisy action chunk and diffusion timestep enter forward. The role encoder and graph are active at every denoising step; auxiliary dynamics are trained and checkpointed but not run by the fixed sampler. Predicted passive roles are soft learned conditioning, not a constraint or an API completion signal. There is no inference ensemble ranking or online safety score.

At early takeover continue closed-grip red descent, then opening and retreat; never infer that red is passive simply because this skill is named transfer_blue. During blue pickup/carry red's current supported pose is the protected baseline. After blue placement, opening and empty-hand retreat must not drag it away. Continue through red approach/closure and initial lift if the inference API keeps this policy active in the expanded overlap. A useful exit is blue actually supported near its world goal while measured red/TCP association and rising red motion indicate retrieval has begun with closed fingers. The successor receives the recent raw state history, including measured blue pose, red pose, TCP, aperture, velocities and goals; no hidden phase or model-only passive score is required. Contact is inferred rather than guaranteed. A baseline may be reconstructed from supported history, but this package neither transmits a cache nor promises recovery after disturbances.

Task success is solely the supplied completion contract: both blocks contained in their respective full rotated-XY goal regions with the specified z tolerance. Skill handoff cues do not add a gripper release, TCP clearance, velocity, or sustained-hold requirement to task success.

## Limits and scientific interpretation

This is a hypothesis implementation, not measured performance. The successful routes provide abundant passive stationarity but no counterfactual collisions, pushes, or recovery. World heads can predict stationarity for dangerous actions; bootstrap agreement does not certify safety and the point-geometry auxiliary cannot repair missing dynamics. Passive labels may err during sliding, partial grasps, contact onset and ambiguous dwell. Two observations do not encode a long-lived baseline and cannot detect old collateral motion if the current pose is adopted as baseline. Limited sites/poses constrain transfer to clutter, more objects, other dynamics or moved goals. Quaternion residuals and geometry handle observed orientations but do not establish rotation invariance.

Dependencies are Torch and appl.public only. There is no file/network access or external kinematics service. Proposed scientific comparisons, not performed here: ablate preservation and clearance separately against the same role-graph DP, remove roles against a comparably sized flat DP, and evaluate action-conditioned forward calibration and collateral motion. Candidate-selection regret is not measurable for this package because candidate ranking is explicitly absent. Interface smoke tests and final training are separate from evaluation; no task-performance claim is made.
